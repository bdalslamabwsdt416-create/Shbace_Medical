### Q1. [Easy] Which pituitary cell type secretes growth hormone?

A. Thyrotroph  
B. Somatotroph  
C. Lactotroph  
D. Corticotroph  
E. Gonadotroph  

**Correct Answer:** B. Somatotroph

**Initial Explanation:**

Growth hormone is secreted by somatotroph cells in the anterior pituitary.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Thyrotrophs secrete thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH).
- **B. Correct.** Somatotrophs produce growth hormone (GH).
- **C. Incorrect.** Lactotrophs secrete prolactin.
- **D. Incorrect.** Corticotrophs secrete adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH).
- **E. Incorrect.** Gonadotrophs secrete follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH).

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- GH is secreted by anterior-pituitary somatotrophs.
- The lecture describes GH as a 191-amino-acid polypeptide.
- The pituitary is controlled by hypothalamic releasing and inhibitory signals.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 4.

### Q2. [Moderate] A random serum GH concentration is measured in the child and is low. Why is a single random GH measurement not sufficient to diagnose GH deficiency?

A. GH secretion is pulsatile, so a random sample may be taken between secretory peaks  
B. GH is secreted only by the thyroid gland  
C. GH is produced only during fetal life  
D. GH is secreted continuously at a constant rate  
E. GH concentration is completely independent of sleep and exercise  

**Correct Answer:** A. GH secretion is pulsatile, so a random sample may be taken between secretory peaks

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture emphasizes that GH secretion is pulsatile. Therefore, a random GH value has limited diagnostic value; stimulation testing is used when deficiency is suspected.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** Pulsatile secretion means a random sample may be low even in a child with normal GH reserve.
- **B. Incorrect.** GH is produced by the anterior pituitary, not the thyroid gland.
- **C. Incorrect.** GH remains important during childhood and adolescence.
- **D. Incorrect.** GH is not secreted at a constant rate.
- **E. Incorrect.** Sleep, stress and exercise influence GH secretion.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- GH secretion is pulsatile.
- A random GH concentration is not a reliable screening test for deficiency.
- GH stimulation/provocation tests assess the secretory reserve.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 4.

### Q3. [Moderate] Which hypothalamic hormone stimulates GH secretion?

A. Growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH)  
B. Somatostatin  
C. Dopamine  
D. Corticotropin-releasing hormone  
E. Thyrotropin-releasing hormone  

**Correct Answer:** A. Growth hormone-releasing hormone (GHRH)

**Initial Explanation:**

GHRH stimulates somatotrophs to release GH, whereas somatostatin inhibits GH secretion.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** GHRH is the principal hypothalamic stimulatory signal for GH secretion.
- **B. Incorrect.** Somatostatin inhibits GH release.
- **C. Incorrect.** Dopamine has an important inhibitory effect on prolactin secretion.
- **D. Incorrect.** CRH stimulates ACTH release.
- **E. Incorrect.** TRH stimulates TSH and can also influence prolactin.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- GHRH stimulates GH.
- Somatostatin inhibits GH.
- GH and IGF-1 participate in negative-feedback regulation of the axis.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 4.

### Q4. [Moderate] Which factor is listed in the lecture as increasing GH secretion?

A. Chronic stimulation of the inhibitory pathway  
B. Sleep  
C. Hyperglycaemia  
D. GH therapy  
E. Somatostatin  

**Correct Answer:** B. Sleep

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture lists sleep, stress and exercise among factors that increase GH secretion. Hypoglycaemia and starvation are also listed.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Stimulation of the inhibitory pathway would reduce, not increase, GH release.
- **B. Correct.** Sleep is a physiological stimulus for GH secretion.
- **C. Incorrect.** Hyperglycaemia is listed as reducing GH secretion.
- **D. Incorrect.** The lecture lists GH therapy among factors that decrease endogenous GH secretion through feedback.
- **E. Incorrect.** Somatostatin inhibits GH release.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Increase GH: sleep, stress, exercise, hypoglycaemia, starvation, arginine and alpha-adrenergic stimulation.

# 2. Growth Hormone Deficiency and Hypopituitarism

## Clinical Case 02 — A toddler with growth failure and recurrent hypoglycaemia

A 2-year-old child is evaluated for poor linear growth, recurrent episodes of hypoglycaemia and prolonged neonatal jaundice. The parents report that the child has a persistently immature facial appearance. Examination also reveals a small phallus. The clinician suspects a pituitary hormone deficiency.

**Case focus:** Isolated GH deficiency, congenital hypopituitarism, neonatal and childhood presentation, and panhypopituitarism.

### Q6. [Moderate] Which statement about normal birth size in a child with isolated GH deficiency is most consistent with the lecture?

A. Birth length and weight may be normal because GH is not essential for intrauterine growth  
B. All affected infants are macrosomic  
C. GH deficiency is diagnosed by low birth weight alone  
D. Severe short length is always present at birth  
E. GH deficiency causes congenital limb shortening in every case  

**Correct Answer:** A. Birth length and weight may be normal because GH is not essential for intrauterine growth

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture notes that children with GH deficiency may have normal length and weight at birth because GH is not essential for fetal growth.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** Normal birth size does not exclude subsequent postnatal GH deficiency.
- **B. Incorrect.** Macrosomia is not the expected feature.
- **C. Incorrect.** Birth weight alone cannot diagnose GH deficiency.
- **D. Incorrect.** Severe short length at birth is not a universal feature of isolated GH deficiency.
- **E. Incorrect.** Congenital limb shortening is not a universal feature of GH deficiency.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Birth size may be normal in GH deficiency.
- Growth failure often becomes apparent during infancy or childhood.
- Growth velocity and serial centile tracking are more informative than a single height.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 6.

### Q7. [Easy] Which neonatal feature is a useful diagnostic clue to congenital hypopituitarism in a male infant?

A. Premature eruption of permanent teeth  
B. Exophthalmos  
C. Micropenis  
D. Macroglossia  
E. Large hands and feet  

**Correct Answer:** C. Micropenis

**Initial Explanation:**

Micropenis is a recognized clue to congenital gonadotropin deficiency and may occur in congenital hypopituitarism. The lecture specifically highlights it in the neonatal presentation.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Delayed rather than premature dentition may occur in hypothyroidism and other growth disorders.
- **B. Incorrect.** Exophthalmos is associated with thyroid eye disease, not congenital hypopituitarism.
- **C. Correct.** Micropenis in a term male infant may indicate deficient pituitary gonadotropin stimulation.
- **D. Incorrect.** Macroglossia is a classical feature of congenital hypothyroidism.
- **E. Incorrect.** Large hands and feet are not the characteristic neonatal clue.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Neonatal hypopituitarism may present with persistent hypoglycaemia, prolonged jaundice, apnoea/cyanosis and micropenis in boys.
- Midline facial defects may coexist.
- Evaluate for deficiencies of other pituitary hormones, not GH alone.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 6.

### Q8. [Moderate] A 6-year-old with GH deficiency has short stature, increased subcutaneous fat and delayed bone maturation. Which additional finding is most consistent with the lecture?

A. Severe intellectual disability is always present

The lecture describes a childish facial appearance, small face/nose/mandible, wide anterior fontanelle in infancy, fine hair and increased subcutaneous fat in children with GH deficiency.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** The lecture states that children with GHD generally have normal mentality and normal IQ.
- **B. Incorrect.** Acromegalic features reflect excess GH, not deficiency.
- **C. Incorrect.** Exophthalmos suggests thyroid eye disease.
- **D. Correct.** An immature facial appearance with small facial structures is described.
- **E. Incorrect.** Palmar-crease hyperpigmentation is a clue to primary adrenal insufficiency.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Childhood GHD: reduced growth velocity, short stature and delayed skeletal maturation.
- Increased subcutaneous fat/relative truncal adiposity may occur.
- Mentality and IQ are generally normal in isolated GHD.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 6.

### Q9. [Hard] A child has growth hormone deficiency together with low TSH and low ACTH. What term is used when all anterior pituitary hormones are deficient?

A. Isolated GH resistance  
B. Cushing syndrome  
C. Panhypopituitarism  
D. Primary hypothyroidism  
E. Graves disease  

**Correct Answer:** C. Panhypopituitarism

**Initial Explanation:**

Panhypopituitarism refers to deficiency of all anterior pituitary hormones. The lecture distinguishes isolated GH deficiency from combined pituitary hormone deficiency.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** GH resistance is a defect in GH action, not a deficiency of all anterior pituitary hormones.
- **B. Incorrect.** Cushing syndrome results from prolonged glucocorticoid excess.
- **C. Correct.** Deficiency of multiple anterior pituitary hormones, potentially including GH, TSH and ACTH, is described as panhypopituitarism.
- **D. Incorrect.** Primary hypothyroidism originates in the thyroid gland and usually produces high TSH.
- **E. Incorrect.** Graves disease is autoimmune hyperthyroidism.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Isolated GHD affects GH alone.
- Panhypopituitarism involves multiple anterior pituitary hormone deficiencies.
- If panhypopituitarism is present, replacement of other deficient hormones is required as well as GH when indicated.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 5.

## Clinical Case 03 — A child with low IGF-1 and unexpectedly high GH

A 5-year-old child has severe short stature and poor growth velocity. Laboratory assessment shows low IGF-1 despite high circulating GH. The clinician suspects a defect in GH action rather than failure of pituitary secretion.

**Case focus:** Causes of hypopituitarism, isolated versus combined deficiency, Laron syndrome, investigations and treatment.

### Q10. [Moderate] Which finding most strongly suggests Laron syndrome rather than pituitary GH deficiency?

A. High IGF-1 with excessive GH action  
B. Low GH with low IGF-1  
C. Low cortisol with high ACTH  
D. High GH with low IGF-1 due to GH receptor resistance  
E. High TSH with low free T4  

**Correct Answer:** D. High GH with low IGF-1 due to GH receptor resistance

**Initial Explanation:**

Laron syndrome is caused by defective GH receptors, resulting in GH insensitivity. GH levels may be high while IGF-1 remains low.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** This suggests excessive GH effect rather than resistance.
- **B. Incorrect.** Low GH and low IGF-1 are more consistent with pituitary GH deficiency.
- **C. Incorrect.** This pattern is consistent with primary adrenal insufficiency.
- **D. Correct.** High GH with low IGF-1 is the characteristic pattern described for GH receptor resistance.
- **E. Incorrect.** This is a pattern of primary hypothyroidism.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Laron syndrome: GH receptor defect → GH insensitivity.
- GH may be high while IGF-1 is low.
- The lecture notes recombinant IGF-1 therapy as an option in selected GH-resistant patients.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 5.

### Q11. [Moderate] Which investigation is used to assess GH secretory reserve in a child with suspected GH deficiency?

A. GH stimulation/provocation test  
B. Thyroid ultrasound  
C. Water deprivation test  
D. Serum calcium alone  
E. Random urine cortisol alone  

**Correct Answer:** A. GH stimulation/provocation test

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture recommends a GH stimulation test using secretagogues such as insulin, glucagon, clonidine, arginine or exercise to assess GH reserve.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** A provocation test evaluates the ability of the pituitary to release GH.
- **B. Incorrect.** Thyroid ultrasound assesses thyroid anatomy.
- **C. Incorrect.** Water deprivation testing is used in the evaluation of diabetes insipidus.
- **D. Incorrect.** Serum calcium does not assess GH secretion.
- **E. Incorrect.** Urinary cortisol is used in the assessment of Cushing syndrome, not GH reserve.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- GH stimulation testing assesses GH secretory reserve.
- IGF-1 and IGF-binding protein may be low in GHD.
- IGF-1 is influenced by liver disease and malnutrition, so results require clinical context.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Pages 6–7.

# 3. Thyroid Hormone Physiology and Congenital Hypothyroidism

## Clinical Case 04 — A newborn identified by thyroid screening

A term newborn appears normal at birth. At the routine newborn screening visit, thyroid testing is abnormal. The clinician explains that early diagnosis is essential because thyroid hormone is important for brain maturation and skeletal growth.

**Case focus:** Thyroid hormone functions, hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis, thyroid hormone synthesis, congenital hypothyroidism causes, screening and early treatment.

### Q14. [Easy] Which is an important function of thyroid hormones during early life?

A. Inhibition of all skeletal growth  
B. Maturation of the brain during the first years of life  
C. Complete inhibition of basal metabolic rate  
D. Prevention of all pituitary hormone release  
E. Suppression of intestinal motility  

**Correct Answer:** B. Maturation of the brain during the first years of life

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture emphasizes the importance of thyroid hormones in brain maturation during the first years of life, as well as normal skeletal growth and metabolic activity.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Thyroid hormones support normal skeletal growth.
- **B. Correct.** Thyroid hormone is essential for early brain development.
- **C. Incorrect.** Thyroid hormones increase basal metabolic rate.
- **D. Incorrect.** They participate in feedback regulation but do not suppress all pituitary hormone release.
- **E. Incorrect.** Thyroid hormones stimulate gut motility.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Thyroid hormone: brain maturation, skeletal growth, increased BMR, cardiac inotropic/chronotropic effects, gut motility and thermoregulation.
- Untreated congenital hypothyroidism can cause irreversible neurodevelopmental harm.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 8.

### Q15. [Moderate] Which sequence correctly describes the hypothalamic–pituitary–thyroid axis?

A. ACTH → thyroid → cortisol  
B. Hypothalamic TRH → pituitary TSH → thyroid T4/T3  
C. TSH → hypothalamus → TRH → thyroid  
D. GH → thyroid → IGF-1  
E. ADH → pituitary TSH → thyroid  

**Correct Answer:** B. Hypothalamic TRH → pituitary TSH → thyroid T4/T3

**Initial Explanation:**

The hypothalamus releases TRH, which stimulates the anterior pituitary to release TSH. TSH stimulates the thyroid gland to produce T4 and T3.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** ACTH stimulates the adrenal cortex, not the thyroid.
- **B. Correct.** This is the sequence shown in the lecture.
- **C. Incorrect.** The direction of the axis is reversed.
- **D. Incorrect.** GH stimulates IGF-1 production, mainly in the liver.
- **E. Incorrect.** ADH regulates water balance and does not drive the thyroid axis.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- TRH stimulates TSH.
- TSH stimulates thyroid hormone synthesis and release.
- T3/T4 provide negative feedback to the hypothalamus and pituitary.
- T4 is converted to active T3 in peripheral tissues.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 8.

### Q16. [Moderate] Which enzyme is essential for oxidation and organification of iodide during thyroid hormone synthesis?

A. Thyroid peroxidase  
B. 5-alpha reductase  
C. Aromatase  
D. 21-hydroxylase  
E. 11-beta hydroxylase  

**Correct Answer:** A. Thyroid peroxidase

**Initial Explanation:**

Thyroid peroxidase catalyses oxidation of iodide and organification of iodine onto tyrosyl residues in thyroglobulin, allowing formation of MIT and DIT and subsequent T3/T4 synthesis.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** Thyroid peroxidase is central to iodination and coupling reactions in thyroid hormone synthesis.
- **B. Incorrect.** 5-alpha reductase converts testosterone to dihydrotestosterone.
- **C. Incorrect.** Aromatase converts androgens to estrogens.
- **D. Incorrect.** 21-hydroxylase is an adrenal steroid synthesis enzyme.
- **E. Incorrect.** 11-beta hydroxylase is involved in adrenal steroid synthesis.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Iodide is oxidized and attached to tyrosine residues in thyroglobulin.
- MIT + DIT forms T3.
- DIT + DIT forms T4.
- Thyroid peroxidase is the key enzyme in the organification/coupling steps.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 9.

### Q17. [Easy] What is the most common cause of congenital hypothyroidism in the lecture?

A. Adrenal insufficiency

D. Graves disease  
E. Thyroid dysgenesis

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

### Q21. [Hard] A newborn has low free T4 and high TSH. Which level of hypothyroidism is most likely?

A. Hyperthyroidism  
B. Primary hypothyroidism  
C. Tertiary hypothyroidism  
D. Thyroid hormone resistance with normal TSH  
E. Secondary hypothyroidism  

**Correct Answer:** B. Primary hypothyroidism

**Initial Explanation:**

In primary hypothyroidism, the thyroid gland fails to produce sufficient hormone, so free T4 is low and pituitary TSH rises in response.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Hyperthyroidism generally produces elevated thyroid hormone with suppressed TSH in primary thyroid hyperfunction.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Primary: low T3/free T4, high TSH.
- Secondary: low T3/free T4, low TSH.
- Tertiary: low TRH leading to low TSH and low thyroid hormone.
- TRH stimulation testing may help distinguish secondary from tertiary disease in selected cases.

**Source:** Endocrine sheet — Page 11.

# 42. Secondary Adrenal Insufficiency — Biochemical Differentiation

## Clinical Case 46 — A child with pituitary disease and low cortisol

A 12-year-old child with a known pituitary lesion develops fatigue, nausea and recurrent hypoglycaemia. Serum cortisol is low, but there is no hyperpigmentation or hyperkalaemia. The clinician distinguishes secondary adrenal insufficiency from primary Addison disease.

### Q187. [Hard] Which biochemical pattern is more consistent with secondary adrenal insufficiency due to pituitary ACTH deficiency?

A. Low cortisol with low or inappropriately normal ACTH; aldosterone is often relatively preserved  
B. High cortisol with suppressed ACTH  
C. High aldosterone with severe hyperkalaemia  
D. High free T4 with suppressed TSH  
E. Low cortisol with markedly high ACTH, hyperkalaemia and hyperpigmentation in every case  

**Correct Answer:** A. Low cortisol with low or inappropriately normal ACTH; aldosterone is often relatively preserved

**Initial Explanation:**

Secondary adrenal insufficiency is caused by inadequate ACTH from pituitary disease or suppression after long-term glucocorticoid therapy. ACTH is low or inappropriately normal, and aldosterone is usually preserved because it is mainly regulated by the renin–angiotensin system and potassium.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** This is the biochemical pattern of secondary adrenal insufficiency.
- **B. Incorrect.** High cortisol with low ACTH suggests an ACTH-independent cortisol excess state.
- **C. Incorrect.** This is not the pattern of secondary adrenal failure.
- **D. Incorrect.** This is a hyperthyroid biochemical pattern.
- **E. Incorrect.** This describes primary Addison disease with high ACTH, often hyperpigmentation and mineralocorticoid deficiency.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Primary adrenal insufficiency: low cortisol, high ACTH, often low aldosterone, hyponatraemia and hyperkalaemia.
- Secondary: low cortisol with low/inappropriately normal ACTH; potassium is often normal because aldosterone is relatively preserved.
- History of pituitary disease or prolonged glucocorticoid therapy supports a secondary cause.
