**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

## Clinical Case 05 — A thriving infant with frequent regurgitation

### Q12. [Easy] Which term best describes the effortless return of small amounts of milk into the mouth in an otherwise thriving infant?

A. Dysphagia  
B. Bilious vomiting  
C. Haematemesis  
D. Projectile vomiting  
E. Regurgitation or posseting  

**Correct Answer:** E. Regurgitation or posseting

**Initial Explanation:**

Regurgitation is the non-forceful return of gastric contents into the oesophagus or mouth. Posseting refers to small amounts of milk returning, often with swallowed air.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Dysphagia means difficulty swallowing.
- **B. Incorrect.** Bilious vomiting suggests bile-stained contents and may indicate intestinal obstruction.
- **C. Incorrect.** Haematemesis is vomiting blood.
- **D. Incorrect.** Projectile vomiting is forceful and is classically associated with hypertrophic pyloric stenosis.
- **E. Correct.** The history describes non-forceful regurgitation/posseting.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Vomiting: forceful ejection of gastric contents.
- Regurgitation: non-forceful return of gastric contents.
- Posseting: small-volume, non-forceful milk return, common in infancy.

**Source:** GIT sheet — Page 6.

### Q13. [Moderate] The infant is growing normally and has no troublesome symptoms or complications. How should the condition be classified?

A. Severe erosive oesophagitis

D. Gastric outlet obstruction  
E. Physiological gastro-oesophageal reflux (GER)

- **B. Incorrect.** Oesophageal atresia typically presents in the newborn with excessive secretions and choking during the first feed.
- **C. Incorrect.** GERD implies troublesome symptoms or complications, such as poor weight gain, oesophagitis or respiratory manifestations.
- **D. Incorrect.** Gastric outlet obstruction causes persistent vomiting and other obstructive features.
- **E. Correct.** Normal growth and absence of complications support physiological GER.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- GER: reflux without troublesome symptoms or complications.
- GERD: reflux associated with troublesome symptoms or complications.
- Most infant reflux resolves spontaneously by 12–24 months, according to the lecture.
