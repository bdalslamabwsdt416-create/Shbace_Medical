### Q1. [Easy] Which statement best defines a vaccine?

A. A preparation used only to treat established viral infection  
B. An antibiotic that directly kills bacteria in the bloodstream  
C. A substance that stimulates the body's immune response  
D. A substance that suppresses all immune responses  
E. A product that provides immediate passive antibodies in every case  

**Correct Answer:** C. A substance that stimulates the body's immune response

**Initial Explanation:**

The source defines a vaccine as a substance that stimulates the body's immune response. Vaccines are used to induce active immunity and reduce the risk or severity of infectious disease.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Vaccines are primarily preventive immunizing agents, not treatment for established viral illness.
- **B. Incorrect.** Antibiotics treat susceptible bacterial infections; they are not vaccines.
- **C. Correct.** This matches the definition in the source.
- **D. Incorrect.** Vaccination stimulates an antigen-specific immune response rather than suppressing immunity.
- **E. Incorrect.** Passive immunization provides ready-made antibodies; it is different from active vaccination.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Vaccine = substance that stimulates the immune response.
- Active immunization stimulates the recipient's own immune response.
- Do not confuse vaccines with immunoglobulin or other passive antibody preparations.

**Source:** Pediatric Vaccination sheet/PDF - Page 2.
