# 1. Approach to a child with anaemia

## Clinical Case 01 - Pallor in a school-aged child

An 8-year-old child is brought because of progressive pallor and reduced exercise tolerance. A full blood count confirms a haemoglobin concentration below the expected range for age. The clinician reviews the basic red-cell indices and reticulocyte count before selecting further tests.

A. Haemoglobin below 14 g/dL  
D. Haemoglobin below 10 g/dL  
B. Haemoglobin below 16 g/dL  
C. Haemoglobin below 12.5 g/dL  

**Correct Answer:** E. Haemoglobin below 11 g/dL

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture defines anaemia in children aged 1-12 years as haemoglobin below 11 g/dL.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** The sheet gives this threshold for neonates, not school-aged children.
- **B. Incorrect.** This is not the stated paediatric threshold.
- **C. Incorrect.** This is not the threshold provided for this age group.
- **D. Incorrect.** The sheet gives this threshold for infants aged 1-12 months.
- **E. Correct.** This is the age-related threshold stated in the course sheet for children aged 1-12 years.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Interpret haemoglobin using the child's age.
- The lecture lists Hb <14 g/dL in neonates, <10 g/dL at 1-12 months and <11 g/dL at 1-12 years.
- Do not apply one haemoglobin cut-off to every paediatric age group.

# 2. Nutritional iron deficiency in infancy

## Clinical Case 02 - A toddler with pallor and a restricted diet

A 15-month-old child is brought with pallor, poor appetite and reduced activity. The child drinks large amounts of unmodified cow's milk and eats little iron-rich food. The family reports that the child sometimes eats soil from plant pots.

### Q5. [Easy] Which diagnosis is most likely?
B. Iron deficiency anaemia  
D. Vitamin B12 deficiency  
C. Hereditary spherocytosis  
E. Sickle cell disease  
A. Aplastic anaemia  

**Correct Answer:** B. Iron deficiency anaemia

**Initial Explanation:**

Iron deficiency is the most common anaemia in childhood, peaks between 6 and 24 months, and is associated with low-iron diets and excessive cow's-milk intake.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Aplastic anaemia causes pancytopenia rather than an isolated nutritional microcytic picture.
- **B. Correct.** The age, restricted iron intake, excessive cow's milk, pallor and pica strongly support iron deficiency.
- **C. Incorrect.** Hereditary spherocytosis typically causes haemolysis, jaundice and splenomegaly.
- **D. Incorrect.** B12 deficiency is associated with macrocytosis and may cause neurological manifestations.
- **E. Incorrect.** Sickle cell disease is a chronic haemolytic disorder with characteristic crises.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Iron deficiency is the commonest paediatric anaemia in the sheet.
- Peak age: 6-24 months.
- Excessive cow's milk and poor complementary feeding are important dietary risks.
- Pica may be a clinical clue.

**Source:** Haematology sheet/PDF - Pages 6-8.

# 3. Confirming and treating iron deficiency anaemia

## Clinical Case 03 - Microcytic hypochromic anaemia

A 20-month-old child has pallor, tachycardia and a soft flow murmur. The full blood count shows low haemoglobin, low MCV, low MCH and low MCHC. The blood film shows microcytes with anisocytosis and poikilocytosis.

### Q9. [Easy] Which red-cell pattern is expected in this child?
A. Microcytic hypochromic anaemia  
C. Macrocytic normochromic anaemia  
D. Normocytic normochromic anaemia  
B. Isolated reticulocytosis without anaemia  
E. Pancytopenia with a normal red-cell size  

**Correct Answer:** A. Microcytic hypochromic anaemia

**Initial Explanation:**

Iron deficiency classically produces microcytic hypochromic anaemia with reduced MCV, MCH and MCHC.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** The indices described are the typical pattern of iron deficiency in the lecture.
- **B. Incorrect.** The child has anaemia with abnormal red-cell indices, not isolated reticulocytosis.
- **C. Incorrect.** Macrocytosis suggests a different group of disorders, including megaloblastic anaemia.
- **D. Incorrect.** The MCV is reduced, so the anaemia is not normocytic.
- **E. Incorrect.** The case describes an isolated microcytic pattern, not pancytopenia.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- IDA: low MCV, low MCH and low MCHC.
- A reactive thrombocytosis may also occur.
- Blood film may show microcytes, anisocytosis and poikilocytosis.

**Source:** Haematology sheet/PDF - Pages 8-10.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

# 6. Folate deficiency and megaloblastic anaemia

## Clinical Case 06 - Macrocytosis in a child with poor nutrition

A 7-year-old child presents with fatigue and pallor. The full blood count shows macrocytic anaemia. The blood film shows large red cells and hypersegmented neutrophils. Dietary history reveals a very restricted diet.

**Correct Answer:** E. Megaloblastic anaemia

**Initial Explanation:**

Megaloblastic anaemia is associated with folate or vitamin B12 deficiency and produces macrocytes with hypersegmented neutrophils.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Megaloblastic anaemia results from folate or B12 deficiency.
- Blood film: macrocytes and hypersegmented neutrophils.

### Q160. [Hard] Which approach is most appropriate to establish and stage suspected childhood lymphoma?

- **E. Incorrect.** Empirical iron therapy must not replace investigation of a suspected malignancy.

- Imaging is used for staging and response assessment.
