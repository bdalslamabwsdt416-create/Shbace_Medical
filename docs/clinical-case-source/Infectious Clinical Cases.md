# 1. Measles

## Clinical Case 01 - A child develops fever, cough, coryza and a spreading rash

A 3-year-old unvaccinated child is brought with high fever, cough, coryza and conjunctivitis. Small white lesions are visible on the buccal mucosa. A maculopapular rash begins behind the ears and on the face, then spreads to the trunk and limbs over the following day.

### Q1. [Easy] Which diagnosis best explains this presentation?

B. Measles  
C. Rubella  
A. Roseola infantum  
E. Erythema infectiosum  
D. Scarlet fever  

**Correct Answer:** B. Measles

**Initial Explanation:**

Measles classically causes a febrile prodrome with cough, coryza and conjunctivitis, followed by Koplik spots and a spreading maculopapular rash.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **B. Correct.** The three Cs, Koplik spots and cephalocaudal rash progression are characteristic of measles.
- **C. Incorrect.** Rubella is usually milder and is strongly associated with postauricular/occipital lymphadenopathy.
- **A. Incorrect.** Roseola typically causes several days of high fever followed by rash as the fever resolves.
- **E. Incorrect.** Parvovirus B19 causes a slapped-cheek appearance and a lacy/reticular rash.
- **D. Incorrect.** Scarlet fever causes a sandpaper-like rash with streptococcal pharyngitis and strawberry tongue.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Measles: fever + cough + coryza + conjunctivitis.
- Koplik spots are a characteristic early oral finding.
- Rash usually begins behind the ears/face and spreads downwards.
- Lack of vaccination increases the likelihood.

**Source:** Infectious Diseases sheet/PDF - Page 3.

### Q2. [Moderate] Which oral finding is most characteristic of measles?

E. Koplik spots on the buccal mucosa  
D. Strawberry tongue  
A. Vesicles on the hard palate  
B. Aphthous ulcers on the gingiva  
C. Gray adherent pharyngeal membrane  

**Correct Answer:** E. Koplik spots on the buccal mucosa

**Initial Explanation:**

Koplik spots are small whitish lesions on the buccal mucosa and are described in the sheet as a pathognomonic clue to measles.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **E. Correct.** Koplik spots strongly support measles in a child with the typical prodrome.
- **D. Incorrect.** Strawberry tongue is associated with scarlet fever.
- **A. Incorrect.** Vesicles and ulcers on the palate may occur with HSV or enterovirus infections.
- **B. Incorrect.** Aphthous ulcers are not the classic measles enanthem.
- **C. Incorrect.** An adherent gray membrane suggests diphtheria, not measles.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Koplik spots: buccal mucosa, early measles clue.
- They may be easier to identify before the exanthem is fully developed.
- Strawberry tongue is a different clinical sign.

**Source:** Infectious Diseases sheet/PDF - Page 3.

# 2. Rubella and Congenital Rubella Syndrome

## Clinical Case 02 - A mildly febrile child has postauricular lymphadenopathy and rash

A 7-year-old child develops a low-grade fever and a fine maculopapular rash. Examination reveals enlarged postauricular and occipital lymph nodes. The child is otherwise relatively well. The family asks about the risks if the same infection occurs during pregnancy.

### Q5. [Easy] Which diagnosis is most likely?

D. Rubella  
B. Measles  
C. Mumps  
A. Scarlet fever  
E. Varicella  

**Correct Answer:** D. Rubella

**Initial Explanation:**

Rubella is usually a mild illness characterized by low-grade fever, maculopapular rash and postauricular/occipital lymphadenopathy.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **D. Correct.** Mild systemic illness with postauricular/occipital lymphadenopathy is characteristic.
- **B. Incorrect.** Measles usually has a more prominent febrile prodrome with cough, coryza, conjunctivitis and Koplik spots.
- **C. Incorrect.** Mumps is characterized by painful parotid swelling.
- **A. Incorrect.** Scarlet fever is associated with streptococcal sore throat, strawberry tongue and a sandpaper rash.
- **E. Incorrect.** Varicella causes crops of pruritic vesicles at different stages.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Rubella is often milder than measles.
- Postauricular and occipital lymphadenopathy is a useful clue.
- Congenital infection is the major concern in pregnancy.

### Q7. [Hard] A woman infected with rubella during pregnancy delivers an infant with sensorineural hearing loss, cataract and patent ductus arteriosus. Which diagnosis best explains this combination?

- **D. Incorrect.** CMV classically causes periventricular calcifications, microcephaly and hearing loss, but cataract/PDA is not the classic combination.
- **E. Incorrect.** Congenital toxoplasmosis is classically associated with chorioretinitis, hydrocephalus and intracranial calcifications.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Congenital rubella: SNHL + cataract + CHD (PDA/pulmonary stenosis).
- Other findings: IUGR, thrombocytopenia, blueberry-muffin rash.
- Maternal infection during pregnancy is the key risk context.

- Pain increases with chewing and sour foods.

# 4. Roseola Infantum

## Clinical Case 04 - An infant develops a rash as a high fever settles

A 10-month-old infant has had high fever for three days but remains relatively well between fever spikes. On the fourth day, the temperature settles and a pink maculopapular rash appears, mainly over the trunk. The infant has no preceding prominent respiratory prodrome.

### Q13. [Easy] Which diagnosis best fits the sequence of fever followed by rash as the fever resolves?

C. Roseola infantum  
E. Measles  
D. Scarlet fever  
B. Rubella  
A. Varicella  

**Correct Answer:** C. Roseola infantum

**Initial Explanation:**

Roseola (sixth disease/exanthem subitum) is associated with HHV-6/7. The child develops several days of high fever, then a maculopapular rash appears as the fever resolves.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **C. Correct.** Fever resolution followed by a trunk-predominant rash is characteristic.
- **E. Incorrect.** Measles rash appears after a prodrome with cough, coryza and conjunctivitis.
- **D. Incorrect.** Scarlet fever is associated with sore throat, strawberry tongue and a sandpaper rash.
- **B. Incorrect.** Rubella often has postauricular/occipital lymphadenopathy with mild fever.
- **A. Incorrect.** Varicella causes pruritic vesicles in crops at different stages.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Roseola: HHV-6/7, sixth disease.
- High fever for several days, then rash as fever settles.
- Rash is often trunk-predominant and fades relatively quickly.
