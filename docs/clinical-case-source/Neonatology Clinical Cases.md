### Q1. [Easy] The neonatal period is defined as which time interval?

A. The first 6 weeks of life  
B. The first 28 days of life  
C. The first 14 days of life  
D. The first 7 days of life  
E. The first 3 months of life  

**Correct Answer:** B. The first 28 days of life

**Initial Explanation:**

The neonatal period covers the first 28 days after birth. The lecture divides it into early neonatal life (birth to day 7) and late neonatal life (day 7 to day 28).

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Six weeks extends beyond the neonatal period.
- **B. Correct.** The neonatal period is the first 28 days of life.
- **C. Incorrect.** Two weeks is not the definition used in the lecture.
- **D. Incorrect.** The first 7 days are the early neonatal period, not the entire neonatal period.
- **E. Incorrect.** Three months is well beyond the neonatal period.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Neonatal period: birth to 28 completed days.
- Early neonatal period: birth to the end of day 7.
- Late neonatal period: day 7 to day 28.
- The term *neonate* refers to an infant aged 28 days or younger.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 3.

### Q2. [Moderate] A baby is born before completing 37 weeks of gestation. How should the baby be classified?

A. Small for gestational age  
B. Preterm  
C. Post-mature  
D. Post-term  
E. Term  

**Correct Answer:** B. Preterm

**Initial Explanation:**

A preterm baby is born before completing 37 weeks of gestation. A term baby is delivered from 37 completed weeks through 41 weeks in the lecture, while post-term is 42 weeks or more.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** SGA is defined by birth weight below the 10th centile for gestational age, not by gestational age alone.
- **B. Correct.** Birth before 37 completed weeks is preterm.
- **C. Incorrect.** Post-maturity is a clinical description associated with prolonged gestation, not birth before 37 weeks.
- **D. Incorrect.** Post-term refers to delivery at or beyond 42 weeks.
- **E. Incorrect.** Term begins at 37 completed weeks.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Preterm: <37 completed weeks.
- Term: 37-41 weeks according to the lecture's classification.
- Post-term: ≥42 weeks.
- Gestational age is calculated from the first day of the last menstrual period (LMP), not from the date of conception.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 3.

### Q4. [Moderate] A newborn's weight is at the 7th centile for gestational age. Which classification is correct?

A. Post-term by definition  
B. Small for gestational age (SGA)  
C. Macrosomic by definition  
D. Appropriate for gestational age (AGA)  
E. Large for gestational age (LGA)  

**Correct Answer:** B. Small for gestational age (SGA)

**Initial Explanation:**

SGA is defined as a birth weight below the 10th centile for gestational age. AGA lies between the 10th and 90th centiles, while LGA is above the 90th centile.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Post-term status is based on gestational age, not birth-weight centile.
- **B. Correct.** The 7th centile is below the 10th centile.
- **C. Incorrect.** Macrosomia is an absolute birth-weight concept and is not defined solely by being below the 10th centile.
- **D. Incorrect.** AGA is between the 10th and 90th centiles.
- **E. Incorrect.** LGA is above the 90th centile.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- SGA: birth weight <10th centile for gestational age.
- AGA: 10th-90th centile.
- LGA: >90th centile.
- SGA and IUGR are related but not synonymous: SGA is a birth-weight classification, whereas IUGR describes failure to achieve genetic growth potential.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 3.

### Q5. [Hard] A newborn is above the 90th birth-weight centile for gestational age but weighs 4.1 kg. Which statement is most accurate?

A. The baby is AGA because the absolute weight is normal  
B. The baby is SGA because the weight is below 4.5 kg  
C. LGA and macrosomia are interchangeable terms  
D. The baby is LGA because of the centile, even though the weight is below the lecture's 4.5-kg macrosomia threshold  
E. The baby is post-term because the weight is high  

**Correct Answer:** D. The baby is LGA because of the centile, even though the weight is below the lecture's 4.5-kg macrosomia threshold

**Initial Explanation:**

LGA is defined relative to gestational age (>90th centile), while macrosomia is an absolute birth-weight threshold in the lecture (>4.5 kg). They are not interchangeable.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** AGA is based on centiles, not the absolute weight alone.
- **B. Incorrect.** SGA is below the 10th centile, not below a fixed weight.
- **C. Incorrect.** LGA is centile-based; macrosomia is absolute-weight based.
- **D. Correct.** A baby can be LGA without exceeding 4.5 kg.
- **E. Incorrect.** Post-term classification depends on gestational age.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Interpret birth weight using gestational-age centiles.
- LGA: >90th centile.
- The lecture separately describes macrosomia as birth weight >4.5 kg.
- Do not confuse LGA, macrosomia, SGA and IUGR.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Pages 3 and 39.

## Clinical Case 02 - A term newborn undergoes the first head-to-toe examination

A healthy term newborn is examined shortly after delivery. The clinician measures the occipitofrontal circumference, assesses the fontanelles and sutures, checks the eyes and mouth, and distinguishes normal birth-related findings from abnormalities needing referral.

B. 45 ± 2 cm  
C. 25 ± 2 cm  
D. 30 ± 2 cm  
E. 35 ± 2 cm  

**Correct Answer:** E. 35 ± 2 cm

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture gives a normal newborn OFC of approximately 35 ± 2 cm. Head circumference should be measured and plotted using an age- and sex-appropriate chart.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** This is above the approximate normal range.
- **B. Incorrect.** This is markedly above the expected term-newborn OFC.
- **C. Incorrect.** This is far below the expected term-newborn range.
- **D. Incorrect.** This is smaller than the approximate value given.
- **E. Correct.** The lecture lists 35 ± 2 cm.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Term newborn OFC: about 35 ± 2 cm in the lecture.
- Measure accurately and interpret with gestational age and growth charts.
- Abnormal head size or trajectory may suggest microcephaly, macrocephaly or a neurological condition.

### Q7. [Moderate] Which statement about the fontanelles is correct according to the lecture?

A. The anterior fontanel normally closes at birth  
B. The posterior fontanel closes after the anterior fontanel  
C. The anterior fontanel usually closes between 9 and 18 months, while the posterior fontanel closes by approximately 2 months  
D. Both fontanelles normally close at 6 months  
E. The posterior fontanel normally remains open until 18 months  

**Correct Answer:** C. The anterior fontanel usually closes between 9 and 18 months, while the posterior fontanel closes by approximately 2 months

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture states that the anterior fontanel closes at approximately 9-18 months and the posterior fontanel from birth to 2 months.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** The anterior fontanel is normally open at birth.
- **B. Incorrect.** The posterior fontanel closes before the anterior fontanel.
- **C. Correct.** This matches the timings in the lecture.
- **D. Incorrect.** The two fontanelles do not normally close at the same time.
- **E. Incorrect.** The posterior fontanel usually closes much earlier.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Anterior fontanel: closes around 9-18 months.
- Posterior fontanel: closes by about 2 months.

- Cranial sutures may overlap at birth due to moulding.

### Q8. [Moderate] A newborn has a diffuse, soft scalp swelling over the presenting part immediately after delivery. It crosses suture lines and is already visible at birth. Which diagnosis is most likely?

A. Caput succedaneum  
B. Cephalohaematoma  
C. Subgaleal haemorrhage  
D. Hydrocephalus  
E. Craniosynostosis  

**Correct Answer:** A. Caput succedaneum

**Initial Explanation:**

Caput succedaneum is a diffuse subcutaneous fluid swelling over the presenting part. It is present immediately after birth and can cross suture lines.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** Immediate onset, soft consistency and crossing sutures are typical of caput succedaneum.
- **B. Incorrect.** Cephalohaematoma is a subperiosteal blood collection that does not cross suture lines and often appears hours after birth.
- **C. Incorrect.** Subgaleal haemorrhage is a deeper, diffuse blood collection that may cause severe blood loss and shock.
- **D. Incorrect.** Hydrocephalus causes abnormal ventricular enlargement and progressive head enlargement, not a localized scalp swelling.
- **E. Incorrect.** Craniosynostosis is premature fusion of cranial sutures.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Caput succedaneum: subcutaneous oedema, immediate, crosses sutures, usually resolves in days.
- Cephalohaematoma: subperiosteal blood, appears after hours, does not cross sutures, resolves over weeks.
- Subgaleal haemorrhage can cause major blood loss and requires urgent assessment.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 4; birth injury comparison - Page 40.

### Q9. [Easy] Which screening examination should be performed in every newborn to detect conditions such as congenital cataract?

A. Visual acuity chart  
B. Cover test only  
C. Fundoscopy after pupil dilation in every newborn  
D. Red reflex examination  
E. Tonometry  

**Correct Answer:** D. Red reflex examination

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture recommends checking the red reflex in all newborns. An absent or abnormal red reflex requires ophthalmology assessment because it may indicate congenital cataract or another significant ocular abnormality.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** A newborn cannot perform a conventional visual acuity chart.
- **B. Incorrect.** A cover test does not replace red reflex screening.
- **C. Incorrect.** Routine dilated fundoscopy in every newborn is not the specific screening test named in the lecture.
- **D. Correct.** Red reflex screening can identify media opacity and congenital cataract.
- **E. Incorrect.** Tonometry measures intraocular pressure and is not the routine screening described.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Perform red reflex screening in all newborns.
- Absent/asymmetric/abnormal red reflex → urgent ophthalmology review.
- The lecture also notes that pupillary light reflex is present at birth and that newborns do not usually produce tears at birth.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Pages 4-5.

## Clinical Case 03 - A baby becomes cyanosed during feeding but improves when crying

A term newborn develops respiratory distress and cyanosis during feeding and when calm. The infant improves when crying. A nasogastric tube cannot be passed through either nostril. The team suspects an upper-airway congenital obstruction.

### Q10. [Moderate] What is the most likely diagnosis?

A. Unilateral choanal atresia  
B. Laryngomalacia  
C. Tracheoesophageal fistula  
D. Bilateral choanal atresia  
E. Congenital diaphragmatic hernia  

**Correct Answer:** D. Bilateral choanal atresia

**Initial Explanation:**

Newborns are obligate nasal breathers. Bilateral choanal atresia can cause immediate respiratory distress and cyanosis during quiet breathing or feeding, with improvement when the baby cries and breathes through the mouth.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Unilateral choanal atresia often presents later and may be less symptomatic.
- **B. Incorrect.** Laryngomalacia causes inspiratory stridor, typically worse with feeding or supine positioning, but does not prevent passage of an NGT through the nose.
- **C. Incorrect.** Tracheoesophageal fistula may cause choking/cyanosis with feeds but does not produce bilateral nasal obstruction.
- **D. Correct.** Bilateral obstruction produces the classic cyclical cyanosis relieved by crying.
- **E. Incorrect.** CDH causes severe respiratory distress with a scaphoid abdomen and bowel sounds in the chest.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Bilateral choanal atresia: respiratory distress/cyanosis when calm or feeding, improves with crying.
- Inability to pass a nasal catheter/NGT is an important clue.
- Confirm with CT and arrange urgent ENT/surgical management.
- The lecture notes an association with CHARGE syndrome.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 5.

### Q11. [Moderate] Which congenital association is particularly linked to choanal atresia in the lecture?

A. CHARGE association  
B. VACTERL association  
C. CATCH-22  
D. Pierre Robin sequence  
E. TORCH syndrome  

**Correct Answer:** A. CHARGE association

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture states that approximately 60% of choanal atresia cases are associated with other congenital malformations, particularly CHARGE.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** CHARGE includes coloboma, heart defect, choanal atresia, growth/developmental delay, genital abnormalities and ear deformity.
- **B. Incorrect.** VACTERL describes vertebral, anal, cardiac, tracheo-oesophageal, renal and limb anomalies.
- **C. Incorrect.** CATCH-22 is associated with DiGeorge syndrome.
- **D. Incorrect.** Pierre Robin sequence includes micrognathia, glossoptosis and airway obstruction, but is not the named association in the lecture.
- **E. Incorrect.** TORCH refers to congenital infections.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- CHARGE: Coloboma, Heart defect, choanal Atresia, Retarded growth/development, Genital abnormalities, Ear deformity.
- Bilateral choanal atresia is an airway emergency.
- Unilateral disease may be detected later.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 5.

## Clinical Case 04 - A healthy newborn has several skin and hormonal-withdrawal findings

A term newborn has small white papules on the nose, a transient pustular rash on an erythematous base, blue-gray macules over the lumbosacral region and mild breast enlargement. The infant is otherwise well and feeding normally.

### Q12. [Easy] White papules over the nose and cheeks caused by obstruction of sebaceous glands are called:

A. Milia  
B. Transient neonatal pustular melanosis  
C. Miliaria  
D. Erythema toxicum neonatorum  
E. Cutis marmorata  

**Correct Answer:** A. Milia

**Initial Explanation:**

Milia are small white papules caused by retention cysts from obstructed sebaceous glands. They commonly occur on the nose and cheeks and resolve spontaneously.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** Milia are white sebaceous retention cysts.
- **B. Incorrect.** Transient neonatal pustular melanosis heals with brown pigmented macules.
- **C. Incorrect.** Miliaria is due to sweat-gland obstruction, often following thermal stress or over-wrapping.
- **D. Incorrect.** Erythema toxicum is a pustular rash on an erythematous base.
- **E. Incorrect.** Cutis marmorata is a reticular vascular pattern that appears when the baby is cooled.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Milia: white papules, blocked sebaceous glands, spontaneous resolution.
- Miliaria: blocked sweat ducts, associated with heat/over-wrapping.
- Most benign neonatal skin lesions do not require treatment.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 8.

### Q13. [Moderate] A well term newborn develops pustules on an erythematous base over the trunk and face at 2-3 days of age. The fluid contains eosinophils. What is the diagnosis?

A. Erythema toxicum neonatorum  
B. Congenital syphilis  
C. Neonatal herpes simplex infection  
D. Miliaria  
E. Transient neonatal pustular melanosis  

**Correct Answer:** A. Erythema toxicum neonatorum

**Initial Explanation:**

Erythema toxicum is common in term newborns. It usually appears at 2-3 days with pustules on an erythematous base; pustular fluid contains eosinophils and the rash resolves spontaneously.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Correct.** The timing, appearance and eosinophils identify erythema toxicum.
- **B. Incorrect.** Congenital syphilis may cause a systemic illness and characteristic rash, not this typical benign eruption.
- **C. Incorrect.** Neonatal HSV may produce vesicular lesions and systemic disease; it is not a benign eosinophilic pustular eruption.
- **D. Incorrect.** Miliaria consists of sweat-related papules/vesicles, often after heat exposure.
- **E. Incorrect.** Transient neonatal pustular melanosis is characterized by pustules that heal with residual brown macules; smears typically show neutrophils.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Erythema toxicum: common in term infants, onset day 2-3, pustules on erythematous base, eosinophils, self-limited.
- Transient neonatal pustular melanosis: neutrophils and residual hyperpigmented macules.
- A well-appearing infant with classic erythema toxicum needs reassurance, not antibiotics.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 8.

### Q14. [Moderate] Blue-gray, ill-defined macules over the lumbosacral area and buttocks are most consistent with:

A. Infantile haemangioma  
B. Congenital dermal melanocytosis (Mongolian spots)  
C. Cutis marmorata  
D. Port-wine stain  
E. Salmon patch  

**Correct Answer:** B. Congenital dermal melanocytosis (Mongolian spots)

**Initial Explanation:**

Congenital dermal melanocytosis presents as blue-gray macular discoloration, most commonly over the lumbosacral region and buttocks. It is more common in darker-skinned infants and usually fades over months to years.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Infantile haemangiomas are vascular tumours that commonly appear during the first month and later regress.
- **B. Correct.** This is the typical site and appearance of congenital dermal melanocytosis.
- **C. Incorrect.** Cutis marmorata is a reticular mottled vascular pattern provoked by cooling.
- **D. Incorrect.** Port-wine stains are vascular malformations present from birth and usually persist/grow with the child.
- **E. Incorrect.** Salmon patches are pink vascular macules, often on the eyelids, glabella or nape.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Mongolian spots = congenital dermal melanocytosis.
- Document location and appearance to distinguish from bruising or inflicted injury.
- Most fade over the first months to years.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 8.

### Q15. [Hard] Which statement correctly distinguishes a port-wine stain from an infantile haemangioma?

A. Both are always absent at birth and regress completely  
B. Infantile haemangioma never grows after birth  
C. Port-wine stain is caused by sebaceous obstruction; haemangioma is caused by sweat-duct obstruction  
D. Port-wine stain always disappears by the end of the first year  
E. Port-wine stain is a vascular malformation present at birth; infantile haemangioma often appears during the first month and later regresses  

**Correct Answer:** E. Port-wine stain is a vascular malformation present at birth; infantile haemangioma often appears during the first month and later regresses

**Initial Explanation:**

The lecture differentiates a port-wine stain (naevus flammeus), present from birth and growing with the child, from an infantile haemangioma, which often appears during the first month, proliferates and later involutes.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Port-wine stains are present at birth and usually persist.
- **B. Incorrect.** Infantile haemangiomas typically proliferate during infancy before regression.
- **C. Incorrect.** Those mechanisms describe milia and miliaria, not vascular lesions.
- **D. Incorrect.** Port-wine stains generally do not disappear spontaneously.
- **E. Correct.** This is the defining distinction.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Port-wine stain: congenital vascular malformation; may be associated with Sturge-Weber or Klippel-Trenaunay syndromes.
- Infantile haemangioma: often appears in the first month, grows then regresses; treatment is considered when large or function-threatening (e.g. airway/vision).
- The lecture lists oral propranolol for selected problematic infantile haemangiomas.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 9.

### Q16. [Moderate] A newborn has breast enlargement and a small amount of milk discharge during the first week. What is the most appropriate advice?

A. Start antibiotics immediately  
B. Start hormonal treatment  
C. Arrange urgent surgical drainage in all cases

Neonatal breast enlargement and occasional “witch's milk” occur due to withdrawal of maternal hormones. It usually resolves spontaneously within weeks; squeezing should be avoided.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Neonatal gynaecomastia can occur in both sexes.
- “Witch's milk” may occur due to maternal hormone withdrawal.
- Do not squeeze the breast.
- Vaginal mucoid discharge, small vaginal bleeding and neonatal acne may also occur as transient hormonal-withdrawal effects.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 6.

### Q161. [Moderate] Which feature distinguishes unconjugated bilirubin from conjugated bilirubin?

A. Conjugated bilirubin is the main cause of kernicterus  
B. Both forms are lipid-soluble and cannot cross membranes  
C. Unconjugated bilirubin is lipid-soluble and albumin-bound; conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and excreted in bile  
D. Unconjugated bilirubin is excreted directly as pale stool  
E. Unconjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and excreted directly in urine  

**Correct Answer:** C. Unconjugated bilirubin is lipid-soluble and albumin-bound; conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and excreted in bile

**Initial Explanation:**

Unconjugated bilirubin is lipid-soluble and travels bound to albumin. Hepatocytes conjugate it using glucuronyl transferase, producing water-soluble conjugated bilirubin, which is excreted into bile and the intestine.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Free unconjugated bilirubin can cross the blood-brain barrier and cause kernicterus; conjugated bilirubin does not cause kernicterus.
- **B. Incorrect.** The two forms differ in solubility and metabolism.
- **C. Correct.** This is the key biochemical distinction.
- **D. Incorrect.** Conjugated bilirubin enters bile and contributes to stool pigment after intestinal metabolism.
- **E. Incorrect.** Unconjugated bilirubin is not water-soluble and is not directly excreted in urine.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Heme breakdown → biliverdin → unconjugated bilirubin.
- Unconjugated bilirubin is lipid-soluble and albumin-bound.
- Hepatic glucuronyl transferase conjugates bilirubin.
- Conjugated bilirubin is water-soluble and excreted in bile.
- Free unconjugated bilirubin can cross the blood-brain barrier and cause kernicterus.

**Source:** Neonatology sheet - Page 43.

### Q162. [Moderate] Which statement about neonatal unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia is correct?

B. It is always due to biliary obstruction  
C. It carries no risk of neurological injury  
D. It may cause kernicterus when free unconjugated bilirubin crosses the blood-brain barrier  
E. It never occurs in haemolytic disease  

**Correct Answer:** D. It may cause kernicterus when free unconjugated bilirubin crosses the blood-brain barrier

**Initial Explanation:**

Unconjugated bilirubin is lipid-soluble. If albumin-binding capacity is exceeded, free unconjugated bilirubin can cross the blood-brain barrier and deposit in the basal ganglia and brainstem nuclei, causing bilirubin encephalopathy.

**Detailed Explanation:**

- **A. Incorrect.** Pale stools and dark urine suggest conjugated/obstructive jaundice.
- **B. Incorrect.** Unconjugated jaundice has many causes, including haemolysis, prematurity and impaired conjugation.
- **C. Incorrect.** Unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia can cause kernicterus.
- **D. Correct.** This is the mechanism of bilirubin neurotoxicity.
- **E. Incorrect.** Rh and ABO incompatibility are important causes of unconjugated hyperbilirubinaemia.

**HIGH-YIELD POINTS:**

- Unconjugated jaundice: usually yellowish skin; normal urine colour and pigmented stool.
- Conjugated/obstructive jaundice: may produce greenish-yellow colour, dark urine and pale stool.
- Kernicterus risk is associated with free unconjugated bilirubin, not conjugated bilirubin.
- Conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia is always pathological and requires evaluation.
