## CC-0039 — Guillain–Barré syndrome

**Question:** A 9 year old child suffers from an acute onset weakness which initially started in the
lower limbs and was preceeded 3 weeks earlier by a respiratoy tract infection. On
examination there is hypotonia and hyporeflexia of both lower limbs. There is no
history of convulsions.
The most likely diagnosis is;

**Options:**
- A. Brain tumour
- B. Poliomyelitis
- C. Guillan-barre syndrome
- D. Werding hoffman syndrome

**Answer:** C — Guillan-barre syndrome

Clinical explanation:
Progressive symmetric weakness beginning in the legs after a respiratory infection with hypotonia and hyporeflexia is typical of GBS.

Options analysis:
A: brain tumour does not fit the acute ascending peripheral pattern. B: poliomyelitis is often asymmetric. C: correct. D: Werdnig–Hoffmann usually presents in infancy with chronic motor weakness.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: CNS; acute flaccid paralysis and GBS.

High-Yield:
Monitor respiratory function and autonomic instability in suspected GBS.

## CC-0040 — Guillain–Barré syndrome

**Question:** About 12 days after a mild upper respiratory infection, a 12-year-old boy complains
of weakness in his lower extremities. Over several days, the weakness progresses to
include the trunk. On examination, deep tendon reflexes are lost in lower
extremities. Spinal fluid studies are notable for elevated protein only. What is the
most likely diagnosis?

**Options:**
- A. Botulism
- B. Post-diphtheritic paralysis . . .
- C. Werdnig Hoffmann disease
- D. Guillain-barre syndrome

**Answer:** D — Guillain-barre syndrome

Clinical explanation:
Ascending weakness after an infection with areflexia and albuminocytologic dissociation (raised CSF protein with no pleocytosis) is characteristic of GBS.

Options analysis:
A: botulism typically causes descending paralysis and pupillary/autonomic findings. B: post-diphtheritic neuropathy follows diphtheria and has different timing/context. C: Werdnig–Hoffmann is chronic/early onset. D: correct.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: CNS; GBS and CSF findings.

High-Yield:
Albuminocytologic dissociation supports GBS, particularly after the first week.

- A4 merged MD; original source: `problem_solving-with-answers.pdf, source p. 18`.

## CC-0042 — Hypertensive encephalopathy

**Question:** A 9 year old child comes to the hospital with an acute onset of generalized
convulsions and disturbed conscious level. the parents did not report any similar
neurological trouble beforehand. On examination, HR was 70/min, RR is 20/min
what is the first action to do after control of convulsions?

**Options:**
- A. Blood gases
- B. Blood pressure measurement
- C. CT brain
- D. Fundus examination

**Answer:** B — Blood pressure measurement

Clinical explanation:
After seizure control, rapidly check reversible causes and vital signs. Severe hypertension can cause hypertensive encephalopathy and seizures; blood pressure is essential to assess.

Options analysis:
A: blood gases may be indicated if respiratory/metabolic compromise is suspected. B: correct among listed choices. C: CT is not the first universal step after stabilization. D: fundus examination is useful later if hypertension/raised ICP is suspected.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Nephrology/CNS; hypertensive emergencies and seizure assessment.

High-Yield:
Always measure blood pressure in a child with a first seizure, after immediate stabilization and glucose assessment.

- A4 merged MD; original source: `problem_solving-with-answers.pdf, source p. 17`.

lower limbs and was preceeded 3 weeks earlier by a respiratoy tract infection. On
examination there is hypotonia and hyporeflexia of both lower limbs. There is no
history of convulsions.

**Options:**
- A. Brain tumour
- B. Poliomyelitis 3
- C. Guillan-barre syndrome
- D. Werding hoffman syndrome - Initially started = there is progression - Hypotonia + hyporeflexia = lower motor neuron lesion - Both LLs = symmetrical disease

**Answer:** C — Guillan-barre syndrome

Clinical explanation:
Symmetric progressive weakness beginning in the lower limbs, hypotonia and hyporeflexia following a respiratory infection are characteristic of acute inflammatory demyelinating polyradiculoneuropathy/GBS. Assess respiratory function and autonomic instability urgently.
