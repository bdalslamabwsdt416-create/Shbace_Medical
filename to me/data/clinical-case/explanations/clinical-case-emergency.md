## CC-0012 — Dehydration, shock & fluid balance

**Question:** An 18 months old boy came to the emergency department with rapid respiration,
drowsiness. He had a history of vomiting and diarrhea for 3 days before the onset of
his condition. By examination HR was 160, RR was 60, Temp. was 38.5 and Bp was
60/40. He had delayed capillary refill. What is the most likely action to be done?

**Options:**
- A. Chest x-ray.
- B. Giving oral ttt and follow up.
- C. Administration of IV fluids.
- D. Blood gas analysis.

**Answer:** C — Administration of IV fluids.

Clinical explanation:
Vomiting/diarrhoea with tachycardia, hypotension, delayed capillary refill and drowsiness indicates shock from severe volume depletion or sepsis. Immediate resuscitation is required.

Options analysis:
A: imaging delays resuscitation. B: oral management is unsafe in shock. C: correct among choices. D: blood gas is useful but does not replace immediate circulation support.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: GIT/Fluid Balance; dehydration and shock.

High-Yield:
Treat shock before pursuing non-urgent diagnostics; reassess after each fluid intervention.

## CC-0056 — Dehydration, shock & fluid balance

**Question:** An 18 months old boy came to the emergency department with
rapid respiration, drowsiness. He had a history of vomiting and
diarrhea for 3 days before the onset of his condition. By
examination HR was 160, RR was 60, Temp. was 38.5 and Bp was
60/40. He had delayed capillary refill.
What is the most likely action to be done?

**Options:**
- A. Chest x-ray
- B. Giving oral ttt and follow up.
- C. Administration of IV fluids.
- D. Blood gas analysis.

**Answer:** C — Administration of IV fluids.

Clinical explanation:
Three days of vomiting/diarrhea with tachycardia, hypotension, tachypnea, drowsiness and delayed capillary refill indicates decompensated shock from fluid loss. Establish IV/IO access and give isotonic crystalloid boluses with frequent reassessment; do not delay resuscitation for imaging or blood gas results.

A — chest radiography does not address shock; B — oral fluids are unsafe/ineffective in a drowsy shocked child; C — correct; D — blood gas may guide subsequent care but must not delay fluid resuscitation.
