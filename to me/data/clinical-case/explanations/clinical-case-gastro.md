## CC-0009 — Pyloric stenosis

**Question:** A 5-week-old boy presents to clinic with vomiting for the last 2 weeks. He is not
gaining weight properly. The mother states the vomiting is projectile, non-bilious
but she feels that he has a good suck and swallow. Examination revealed an olive-
like mass felt to the right of umbilicus .What is the most likely diagnosis?

**Options:**
- A. Early gastroenteritis
- B. Sepsis
- C. Faulty finding technique
- D. pyloric stenosos

**Answer:** D — pyloric stenosos

Clinical explanation:
A 5-week-old with progressive projectile non-bilious vomiting, poor weight gain and an olive-like mass has classic pyloric stenosis.

Options analysis:
A: gastroenteritis usually has diarrhoea/systemic illness. B: sepsis does not explain the classic olive and projectile non-bilious vomiting. C: a finding-technique error is possible but the full clinical pattern supports pyloric stenosis. D: correct.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: GIT/Neonatology; pyloric stenosis.

High-Yield:
Pyloric stenosis causes hypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis; correct fluids/electrolytes before surgery.

## CC-0010 — Pyloric stenosis

**Question:** 3week child coming with vomiting ,non bile staining .loss of weight ........
which of the following present in examination :

**Options:**
- A. Abdominal distention
- B. Abdominal tenderness
- C. Decrease intestinal sound
- D. Olive mass in the upper quadrant

**Answer:** D — Olive mass in the upper quadrant

Clinical explanation:
The described 3-week-old with progressive non-bilious vomiting and weight loss is consistent with hypertrophic pyloric stenosis; a palpable olive is a classic finding.

Options analysis:
A–C are not typical specific findings. D is the characteristic finding.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: GIT/Neonatology; pyloric stenosis.

High-Yield:
The classic triad is projectile non-bilious vomiting, visible gastric peristalsis and palpable olive (not always all present).

## CC-0059 — Intussusception & acute abdominal pain

**Question:** A 12 months old boy presents to the emergency department with a 6 hour history
of vomiting, colicky abdominal pain, and irritability. On physical examination a
sausage like mass is palpable in the right upper quadrant of the abdomen
What is the most appropriate next step in management?

**Options:**
- A. Order a CT scan of the abdomen
- B. Order a barium swallow
- C. Obtain a surgical consultation
- D. Follow up examination after 4 hours

**Answer:** C — Obtain a surgical consultation

Clinical explanation:
The age, episodic colicky pain, irritability, vomiting and sausage-shaped abdominal mass strongly indicate ileocolic intussusception. The child needs urgent assessment; ultrasound is the preferred diagnostic test and pneumatic/hydrostatic enema is usually first-line reduction if stable and no perforation/peritonitis. Surgical consultation is appropriate among the listed choices.
