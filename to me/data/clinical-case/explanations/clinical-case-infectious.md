## CC-0026 — Measles & viral exanthems

**Question:** A 10 months old infant presents with a day history of blanching confluent rash which
started on his face and now covers his entire body. He is miserable with conjunctivitis
and fever of 38 C. the illness started with runny nose and cough 5 days
previously. What is the most likely diagnosis?

**Options:**
- A. Scarlet fever
- B. Sweat rash
- C. Chicken pox
- D. Measles

**Answer:** D — Measles

Clinical explanation:
A prodrome of cough, coryza and conjunctivitis followed by a confluent maculopapular rash beginning on the face and spreading downwards is classic measles.

Options analysis:
A: scarlet fever requires a compatible streptococcal pharyngitis and sandpaper rash. B: miliaria is not a systemic febrile exanthem. C: varicella is vesicular in crops. D: correct.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Infectious Diseases; measles and exanthems.

High-Yield:
Remember the “3 Cs”: cough, coryza and conjunctivitis.

## CC-0027 — Roseola & febrile seizures

**Question:** A 15 months old infant presents to the emergency department with a 4-day history of
high fever without any localizing sign. She suffers self limiting convulsion and is
admitted for observation. The next day the fever subsides, but a red popular rash
develops over her trunk and abdomen. What is the most likely diagnosis:

**Options:**
- A. Measles
- B. Rubella
- C. Roseola
- D. Chicken pox

**Answer:** C — Roseola

Clinical explanation:
High fever for several days followed by defervescence and then a trunk-predominant maculopapular rash is characteristic of roseola (HHV-6/7). Febrile seizures may occur during the fever.

Options analysis:
A: measles rash appears with ongoing fever and begins on the face with the 3 Cs. B: rubella typically has lymphadenopathy and a shorter rash, but the abrupt defervescence pattern points to roseola. C: correct. D: varicella produces vesicles in different stages.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Infectious Diseases; viral exanthems and febrile seizures.

High-Yield:
Roseola rash classically appears as the fever resolves.

## CC-0028 — Measles & viral exanthems

**Question:** An 8 years old girl present with low grade fever and a diffuse maculopapular rash.
On examination her physician notes mild tenderness and marked swelling of her
posterior cervical and occipital lymph nodes. Three days after the onset of illness ,
the rash has vanished. What is the most likely diagnosis ?

**Options:**
- A. Measles.
- B. German measles.
- C. Roseola infantum.
- D. Infectious mononucleosis.

**Answer:** B — German measles.

Clinical explanation:
Low-grade fever, posterior cervical and occipital lymphadenopathy, and a short-lived maculopapular rash are typical of rubella.

Options analysis:
A: measles usually has high fever and the 3 Cs. B: correct. C: roseola mainly affects infants and follows high fever. D: infectious mononucleosis usually has generalized lymphadenopathy, pharyngitis and possible splenomegaly.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Infectious Diseases; rubella and viral exanthems.

High-Yield:
Posterior auricular, posterior cervical and occipital nodes are characteristic clues to rubella.

## CC-0046 — Gastroenteritis & diarrhea

**Question:** A 3 years old boy came to the outpatient's clinic complaining of mild fever, runny,
nose, malaise and vomiting. On throat examination there was hyperemia of the throat.
What is the most likely medicine to be given?
1-Oral Amoxicillin.
2- Paracetamol.
3- Multivitamin.
4-Acetylsalicylic acid.

**Answer status:** No verified selectable answer is encoded; no answer has been guessed.

Clinical explanation:
A mild febrile upper respiratory illness with coryza and pharyngeal hyperaemia is usually viral. Treat discomfort/fever and provide supportive care; antibiotics are not indicated without evidence of bacterial disease.

Options analysis:
1: amoxicillin is not indicated for uncomplicated viral URI. 2: correct. 3: multivitamins do not treat the infection. 4: aspirin should be avoided in children with viral illness because of Reye syndrome association.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Respiratory/Infectious; viral URTI and rational antibiotic use.

High-Yield:
A viral URTI does not need antibiotics; avoid aspirin in children.

## CC-0072 — Roseola & febrile seizures

**Question:** A 15 months old infant presents to the emergency department with a 4-day history of
high fever without any localizing sign. She suffers self limiting convulsion and is
admitted for observation. The next day the fever subsides, but a red popular rash
develops over her trunk and abdomen. What is the most likely diagnosis:

**Options:**
- A. Measles
- B. Rubella
- C. Roseola infantum
- D. Chicken pox

**Answer:** C — Roseola infantum

Clinical explanation:
A young child with several days of high fever without a localizing source who develops a maculopapular rash on the trunk as the fever resolves is typical of roseola, usually due to HHV-6/7. Febrile seizures can occur during the fever phase.
