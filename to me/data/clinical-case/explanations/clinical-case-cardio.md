## CC-0002 — Rheumatic fever & carditis

**Question:** A 12 years old female came to hospital with fever, difficulty in breathing, severe
effort intolerance and joint pain which started in the rt. wrist and later involved the
lt. knee. By examination BP was 90/60, HR was 140, RR was 35 and Temp. was 39 c.
Abdominal examination revealed enlarged tender liver. The first action to be done:

**Options:**
- A. Starting anti failure treatment.
- B. Non steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs administration.
- C. IV fluid administration.
- D. Blood culture .

**Answer:** A — Starting anti failure treatment.

Clinical explanation:
Migratory large-joint arthritis after infection suggests acute rheumatic fever; tachycardia, tachypnoea and tender hepatomegaly indicate decompensated heart failure from carditis. Immediate stabilization and heart-failure management take priority.

Options analysis:
A: correct priority in the described decompensation. B: anti-inflammatory treatment does not correct acute heart failure. C: routine IV fluids may worsen congestion. D: cultures do not take priority over stabilizing heart failure.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: CVS; acute rheumatic fever, carditis and heart failure.

High-Yield:
Treat heart failure first in a child with suspected rheumatic carditis and congestion.

## CC-0003 — Infective endocarditis

**Question:** A previously healthy 8 years old boy has a 3 weeks history of low grade fever of
unknown source, fatigue , myalgia and headaches. On examination, he is found to
have a heart murmur, petechiae and mild splenomegaly. What is the most likely
diagnosis ?

**Options:**
- A. Rheumatic fever.
- B. Scarlet fever.
- C. Endocarditis.
- D. Tuberculosis.

**Answer:** C — Endocarditis.

Clinical explanation:
Prolonged fever, a murmur, petechiae and splenomegaly suggest infective endocarditis. Obtain blood cultures before antibiotics when the child is stable enough, and perform echocardiography.

Options analysis:
A: acute rheumatic fever usually follows a preceding streptococcal infection and has migratory arthritis/carditis. B: scarlet fever is an acute GAS toxin-mediated illness. C: correct. D: TB does not explain the murmur and peripheral endocarditis signs as well.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: CVS/Infectious; infective endocarditis.

High-Yield:
Blood cultures should be obtained before antibiotics when this does not delay emergency treatment.

## CC-0031 — Heart failure in infancy

**Question:** A 3 week old baby, who was full term, Is brought to the hospital. He has recently
been having problems completing his feeds and today appears short of breath. On
examination, his HR was 180/min, RR 72/min, rectal temperature 37.4, BP 80/50,
and he had a 4 cm hepatomegaly. All blood tests were normal. What is the most likely
diagnosis?

**Options:**
- A. Neonatal hepatitis
- B. Respiratory distress syndrome
- C. Heart failure
- D. Congenital infection

**Answer:** C — Heart failure

Clinical explanation:
Tachycardia, tachypnoea, feeding difficulty and hepatomegaly in a 3-week-old infant form a typical heart-failure pattern. Normal routine blood tests do not exclude congenital heart disease or myocardial dysfunction.

Options analysis:
A: hepatitis does not explain the cardiopulmonary pattern as well. B: RDS is usually an illness of prematurity and presents immediately after birth. C: correct. D: no supporting congenital-infection findings are provided.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: CVS and Neonatology; heart failure in infancy.

High-Yield:
In infants, poor feeding, tachypnoea, tachycardia and hepatomegaly can be the main manifestations of heart failure.
