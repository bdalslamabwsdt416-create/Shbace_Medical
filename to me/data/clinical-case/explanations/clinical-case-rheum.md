## CC-0001 — Approach to fever and arthritis

**Question:** A mother brings to the clinic her 4 years old son who began complaining of Rt knee
pain 2 weeks ago, is limping slightly, is fatigued and has had a fever 38.2C
What is the important diagnostic Lab test to perform?

**Options:**
- A. CBC with differential
- B. Sedimentation rate
- C. EBV titre
- D. Rheumatoid factor

**Answer:** A — CBC with differential

Clinical explanation:
A child with fever, fatigue and unilateral knee pain/limp requires evaluation for serious causes including infection and malignancy. CBC is an appropriate first-line test among the choices.

Options analysis:
A: correct initial test. B: ESR is nonspecific. C: EBV titre is not targeted to this presentation. D: rheumatoid factor is not the first test and does not diagnose acute monoarthritis.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: CVS/Rheumatology/Hematology; approach to fever and arthritis.

High-Yield:
In a child with bone/joint pain and systemic symptoms, exclude infection and malignancy early.

## CC-0013 — IgA vasculitis & renal involvement

**Question:** A 5- year- old boy who was previously healthy has a 1-day history of low grade
fever, colicky abdominal pain, and a skin rash on his buttocks. On examination, no
abdominal tenderness, stool is positive for blood and platelet count is normal.
What Is the most likely diagnosis?

**Options:**
- A. Anaphylactoid purpura
- B. Meningococcemia
- C. Leukemia
- D. Hemophilia A

**Answer:** A — Anaphylactoid purpura

Clinical explanation:
Low-grade fever, colicky abdominal pain, buttock rash, gastrointestinal bleeding and normal platelet count strongly suggest IgA vasculitis.

Options analysis:
A: correct. B: meningococcaemia usually causes toxic illness and rapidly progressive purpura. C: leukemia often has cytopenias/systemic findings. D: haemophilia causes bleeding but not characteristic palpable purpura/abdominal vasculitis.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Rheumatology/GIT/Nephrology; IgA vasculitis.

High-Yield:
Normal platelets with palpable purpura helps distinguish vasculitis from thrombocytopenic purpura.

## CC-0023 — IgA vasculitis & renal involvement

**Question:** A 7 year old boy was limping for 3 days presented to the surgical department with
severe acute colicky abdominal pain. The surgery resident asked for medical
consultation for a rash on the back of both lower limbs of the child.
The acute abdomen is due to

**Options:**
- A. Rheumatic fever
- B. Appendicitis
- C. Henoch-schonlein purpura
- D. Rheumatoid arthritis

**Answer:** C — Henoch-schonlein purpura

Clinical explanation:
Palpable purpura on the lower limbs/buttocks with abdominal pain and joint symptoms is characteristic of IgA vasculitis. Gastrointestinal vasculitis can cause colicky pain and bleeding.

Options analysis:
A: rheumatic fever does not explain purpura and acute abdominal symptoms. B: appendicitis does not explain the characteristic rash and arthralgia. C: correct. D: rheumatoid arthritis does not explain the full syndrome.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Rheumatology/Nephrology/Hematology; IgA vasculitis and systemic manifestations.

High-Yield:
Check urinalysis and blood pressure because renal involvement may be silent.

fatigued and has had a fever 38.2C

**Options:**
- A. CBC with differential
- B. Sedimentation rate
- C. EBV titre
- D. Rheumatoid factor

**Answer:** A — CBC with differential

Clinical explanation:
A febrile child with a limp and persistent unilateral knee symptoms needs prompt assessment. CBC can identify cytopenias/blasts or leukocytosis and is the best initial option offered, but it does not diagnose septic arthritis or osteomyelitis. In practice add ESR/CRP, blood cultures and urgent joint imaging/aspiration when indicated.
