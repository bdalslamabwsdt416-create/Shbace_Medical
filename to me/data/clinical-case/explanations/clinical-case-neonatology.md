## CC-0029 — Neonatal jaundice & cholestasis

**Question:** A 4week-old, fullterm, breast fed girl has worsening yellowish discolouration of the
skin, that the parents first noticed 15 days ago. On her examination, she is well
appearing with good suckling and reflex activity, and is noted to have a liver edge4cm
below her costal margin. Her total bilirubin is 12 and direct bilirubin is 9.
What is the most likely diagnosis

**Options:**
- A. Biliary atresia
- B. Cholecystitis
- C. Sepsis
- D. Breast milk jaundice

**Answer:** A — Biliary atresia

Clinical explanation:
At 4 weeks, persistent jaundice with direct bilirubin 9 of total 12 mg/dL is conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia. Hepatomegaly supports cholestasis. This requires urgent evaluation for biliary atresia because timely referral is critical.

Options analysis:
A: best listed diagnosis. B: cholecystitis is not supported by the stem. C: well appearance does not absolutely exclude infection, but no systemic features are given. D: breast-milk jaundice is predominantly unconjugated.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Neonatology; prolonged neonatal jaundice and cholestasis.

High-Yield:
Any conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia in an infant is pathological and warrants prompt work-up.

## CC-0030 — Neonatal jaundice & cholestasis

**Question:** A 3 day old infant presents with the complaint of yellowish skin. Both the mother and
the baby have O +ve blood. The baby's direct serum bilirubin is 0.2 mg/dl. With a total
serum bilirubin of 11.8 mg/dl. The hemoglobin is 17 gm/dl. Platelete count is
278,000/ul. Reticulocyte count is 1.5%. the peripheral smear doesn’t show
abnormalities. The most likely diagnosis:

**Options:**
- A. Rh or ABO incompatibility
- B. Physiologic jaundice
- C. Sepsis
- D. Congenital spherocytic anemia
- E. Biliary atresia

**Answer:** B — Physiologic jaundice

Clinical explanation:
The infant is 3 days old with predominantly unconjugated bilirubin, normal haemoglobin, reticulocyte count and smear, and mother and baby both O positive. These findings support physiologic jaundice rather than immune haemolysis or cholestasis.

Options analysis:
A: no ABO/Rh mismatch or evidence of haemolysis. B: correct. C: no systemic illness or supporting laboratory evidence. D: normal smear argues against spherocytosis. E: biliary atresia causes conjugated jaundice.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Neonatology; neonatal jaundice and haemolysis.

High-Yield:
Interpret bilirubin fraction, age at onset, rate of rise and haemolysis markers together.

## CC-0032 — Neonatal sepsis & meningitis

**Question:** A 2 week old infant develops fever, 38.9 C, vomiting, and irritability. His heart rate is
170/min, and RR is 40/min. The infants anterior fontanelle is full, but there is no nuchal
(neck-related) rigidity. The rest of examination is unremarkable.
What is the appropriate management?

**Options:**
- A. Oral fluid and follow up in 24 hr
- B. Oral amoxicillin and follow up in 1 week
- C. Admission to hospital for investigation and ttt
- D. IM Ceftriaxone and follow up in 1 week

**Answer:** C — Admission to hospital for investigation and ttt

Clinical explanation:
A febrile 2-week-old with vomiting, irritability and a full/bulging fontanelle is at risk of serious bacterial infection, meningitis and sepsis. A neonate/young infant with these features needs urgent hospital assessment and empiric treatment according to protocol.

Options analysis:
A, B and D: outpatient management is inappropriate for this age and presentation. C: correct. The source mentions CT, CBC, cultures and IV antibiotics; imaging and lumbar puncture should be selected according to clinical stability and contraindications, not performed automatically.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Neonatology and Infectious Diseases; neonatal sepsis/meningitis.

High-Yield:
A young febrile infant is high risk even without neck stiffness.

## CC-0033 — Neonatal birth injury

**Question:** A 1 –day- infant who was born by a difficult forceps delivery is alert and active. She
doesn't move her left arm and keeps it internally rotated by her side with the forearm
extended and pronated. Which of the following is an expected clinical finding?
1. Intact both Moro and grasp reflex.
2. Lost both Moro and grasp reflex.
3. Intact Moro and lost grasp reflex
4. Lost Moro and intact grasp reflex.

**Model answer:** 4 — Lost Moro reflex with intact grasp reflex (Erb palsy).

Clinical explanation:
The internally rotated, adducted arm with extended/pronated forearm after difficult delivery is the classic C5–C6 upper brachial plexus injury (Erb palsy). Moro is impaired on the affected side while grasp is usually preserved.

Options analysis:
1: both intact is inconsistent. 2: grasp is usually preserved. 3: reverses the expected pattern. 4: correct.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Neonatology; birth injuries and brachial plexus injury.

High-Yield:
Erb palsy: “waiter’s tip” posture with absent Moro but preserved grasp.

- Q9 · A4 merged MD; original source: `problem_solving-with-answers.pdf, source pp. 3–4`.

## CC-0034 — Neonatal hypothermia & thermal care

**Question:** An infant weighing 1400 gm is born at 32 weeks.HR:140, RR:80, temp:35 C. the lungs
are clear with bilateral breath sounds, there is no murmur.
Which of the following is the most important first step in evaluating this infant:
1. Obtain CBC and differential
2. Perform lumbar puncture
3. Chest x-ray
4. Place infant under warmer
5. Administer oxygen

**Model answer:** 4 — Place the infant under a warmer.

Clinical explanation:
The preterm, low-birth-weight infant is hypothermic at 35°C. Thermal stabilization is an immediate priority; hypothermia can worsen respiratory distress, hypoglycaemia and metabolic instability.

Options analysis:
1–3: investigations should not precede correction of obvious hypothermia. 5: oxygen is indicated only if oxygenation/respiratory status requires it; lungs are clear and no hypoxaemia is stated. 4: correct.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Neonatology; thermal care of preterm infants.

High-Yield:
Prevent heat loss during initial stabilization of every preterm or low-birth-weight infant.

## CC-0035 — Neonatal hypocalcemia in an infant of a diabetic mother

**Question:** A 1 day old infant develops jitteriness and convulsions . He was born at 34 weeks
gestation to a woman whose pregnancy was complicated by diabetes mellitus and
pregnancy induced hypertension. Which of the following serum values could explain
his condition?
1. Serum bicarbonate level of 22 mEq/dl
2. Serum calcium of 6.2 mg/dl
3. Serum glucose of 45 mg/dl
4. Serum sodium of 138 mEq/dl.

**Model answer:** 2 — Serum calcium 6.2 mg/dL.

Clinical explanation:
A symptomatic preterm infant of a diabetic mother is at risk of early neonatal hypocalcaemia. A calcium of 6.2 mg/dL is low and can cause jitteriness and seizures.

Options analysis:
1: bicarbonate 22 is not the key abnormality. 2: low calcium explains the symptoms. 3: glucose 45 mg/dL may require contextual assessment but is not the intended biochemical abnormality. 4: sodium 138 is normal.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Neonatology; infant of diabetic mother and neonatal metabolic complications.

High-Yield:
Check glucose, calcium and magnesium in a symptomatic neonate.

## CC-0036 — Neonatal skin findings

**Question:** A 1 week old infant presents with large pale blue lesion over the buttocks bilaterally.
The lesion is not palpable and it is not warm or tender. The mother denies trauma and
reports that the lesion has been present since birth.
What is the most appropriate action ?

**Options:**
- A. Check platelet count.
- B. Give vitamin K injection.
- C. Delay circumcision.
- D. Reassurance.

**Answer:** D — Reassurance.

Clinical explanation:
A flat, nonpalpable, non-tender blue-grey patch present since birth over the sacral/buttock region is consistent with congenital dermal melanocytosis (Mongolian spot). It is benign and fades over time.

Options analysis:
A/B/C: no evidence of thrombocytopenia, vitamin K deficiency or active bleeding. D: correct.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Dermatology within Pediatrics; newborn skin findings.

High-Yield:
Document congenital dermal melanocytosis to distinguish it from later bruising or inflicted injury.

## CC-0037 — Prematurity & neonatal respiratory disease

**Question:** A newly born infant full term weighs 3.6 kg was born by caeserian section and there
was little amniotic fluid, he cried after birth but later on, after few hours he started to
develop distress. x ray showed fluid line and increased bronchovascular markings
the diagnosis is:
1- RDS
2- Transient tachypnea of newborn
3- Meconium aspiration
4- Pneumonia

**Model answer:** 2 — Transient tachypnoea of the newborn (TTN).

Clinical explanation:
A term infant delivered by caesarean section develops respiratory distress after birth. Fluid in fissures and prominent bronchovascular markings are characteristic of retained fetal lung fluid.

Options analysis:
1: RDS is more typical in prematurity with diffuse reticulogranular/ground-glass changes. 2: correct. 3: meconium aspiration generally has compatible meconium exposure and patchy infiltrates. 4: pneumonia requires supporting infectious findings.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Neonatology and Respiratory; TTN and neonatal respiratory distress.

High-Yield:
TTN is more common after caesarean delivery and usually improves over 24–72 hours.
