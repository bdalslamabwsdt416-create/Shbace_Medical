## CC-0005 — Congenital hypothyroidism

**Question:** A 5-month-old girl presented with history of constipation and delayed
developmental milestones. She had prolonged physiological jaundice. On exam, she
is hypoactive, has an open mouth with large tongue. Other systemic examinations
are within normal.
What is the next step in management?

**Options:**
- A. Checking T4 and TSH
- B. Checking serum bilirubin
- C. Doing CT scan of head
- D. Follow up after 4 weeks

**Answer:** A — Checking T4 and TSH

Clinical explanation:
Constipation, delayed milestones, hypoactivity, macroglossia and prolonged jaundice suggest congenital hypothyroidism. Thyroid function testing is the appropriate next investigation.

Options analysis:
A: correct. B: bilirubin does not investigate the underlying syndrome. C: CT is not first-line. D: delay risks irreversible neurodevelopmental harm.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Endocrine; congenital hypothyroidism.

High-Yield:
Early diagnosis and levothyroxine treatment are crucial for neurodevelopment.

## CC-0006 — Congenital hypothyroidism

**Question:** The mother of a 2 week old infant reports that her baby sleeps most of the day, she
has to awaken her every 4 hours to feed and the infant has persistently hard stool .
On examination, HR 75/m and temp. Is 35 C , baby is still jaundiced and has a
distended abdomen and umbilical hernia. What is the most appropriate diagnostic
test ?

**Options:**
- A. Abdominal ultrasound.
- B. Blood count and blood culture.
- C. Total and direct serum bilirubin.
- D. T4 and TSH.

**Answer:** D — T4 and TSH.

Clinical explanation:
Lethargy, prolonged jaundice, constipation, hypothermia, bradycardia, distended abdomen and umbilical hernia strongly suggest congenital hypothyroidism.

Options analysis:
A: ultrasound does not confirm hypothyroidism. B: infection screen may be indicated if clinically unwell but does not address the classic syndrome. C: bilirubin fractionation is not the best diagnostic test. D: correct.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Endocrine/Neonatology; congenital hypothyroidism and prolonged jaundice.

High-Yield:
Newborn-screening abnormalities require confirmatory serum TSH and free T4 without delay.

## CC-0007 — Diabetes & diabetic ketoacidosis

**Question:** A 7 year old boy arrives at the emergency department, complaining of rapid
breathing and vomiting, dating 3 days ago, he has been receiving IM antibiotics for
3 days with no improvement. On examination, he has rapid deep breathing with RR
60/min, HR 90/min. chest x-ray was normal. What is the next investigation to do?

**Options:**
- A. CT chest
- B. Upper GIT endoscopy
- C. Echocardiography
- D. Blood gases

**Answer:** D — Blood gases

Clinical explanation:
Rapid deep breathing with vomiting and normal chest imaging suggests metabolic acidosis with respiratory compensation, including possible diabetic ketoacidosis. Blood gas and serum ketones/glucose clarify the acid-base status.

Options analysis:
A: chest CT is not indicated with normal CXR and no focal chest signs. B: endoscopy is not an acute next step. C: echocardiography is not suggested by the findings. D: correct.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Endocrine; DKA and acid-base interpretation.

High-Yield:
Kussmaul breathing is a compensatory response to metabolic acidosis.

- A4 merged MD; original source: `problem_solving-with-answers.pdf, source p. 19`.

## CC-0008 — Diabetes & diabetic ketoacidosis

**Question:** A girl presents with frequency of urine difficult breathing. the physician suspects new onset
diabetes and he performs urine analysis and found glucose and ketones in urine
the blood gas of this patient is likely to be

**Options:**
- A. pH: 7.05 CO2: 36 HCO3: 18 ( it is mixed )
- B. pH: 7.36 CO2: 37 HCO3: 22
- C. pH: 7.45 CO2: 50 HCO3: 11
- D. pH: 7.2 CO2: 25 HCo3: 12 (metabolic acidosis)

**Answer:** D — pH: 7.2 CO2: 25 HCo3: 12 (metabolic acidosis)

Clinical explanation:
Glucosuria and ketonuria with deep breathing indicate DKA. The low pH and bicarbonate show metabolic acidosis; the low PaCO₂ is respiratory compensation.

Options analysis:
A: mixed/discordant values and not the intended metabolic-acidosis pattern. B: normal acid-base profile. C: alkalemia with low bicarbonate is internally discordant. D: correct.

Curriculum alignment:
SHETS: Endocrine; DKA and acid-base interpretation.

High-Yield:
DKA is metabolic acidosis with ketonaemia/ketonuria and hyperglycaemia; assess electrolytes and potassium before insulin.

- A4 merged MD; original source: `problem_solving-with-answers.pdf, source p. 19`.
