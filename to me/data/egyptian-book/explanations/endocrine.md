## EB-paediatrics-miscellaneous-topics-accepted-Q127 — Q127 — Endocrinology / precocious puberty

**Source:** Question-bank MD p.162 · printed p.312 · answer-key PDF p.169 · original Q127

**Initial explanation**

Pseudoprecocious (peripheral) puberty results from sex-steroid production independent of normal hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal activation. Secondary sexual characteristics develop, but true gonadal maturation and the normal central pubertal sequence are absent or discordant.

**Detailed explanation**

Pseudoprecocious (peripheral) puberty results from sex-steroid production independent of normal hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal activation. Secondary sexual characteristics develop, but true gonadal maturation and the normal central pubertal sequence are absent or discordant. **Why the others are wrong:** (a) Early development of secondary sexual characters: present. (b) No normal gonadal maturation: characteristic of peripheral/pseudoprecocious puberty. (c) Both: correct. (d) None: incorrect.

Curriculum alignment: Endocrine 2025: precocious puberty, gonadotropin-dependent and independent puberty. **High-yield point / Review Notes:** Differentiate central (GnRH-dependent) from peripheral (GnRH-independent) precocious puberty.

High-yield point: Pseudoprecocious (peripheral) puberty results from sex-steroid production independent of normal hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal activation.

## EB-paediatrics-miscellaneous-topics-accepted-Q128 — Q128 — Endocrinology / growth hormone excess

**Source:** Question-bank MD p.162 · printed p.312 · answer-key PDF p.168 · original Q128

**Initial explanation**

Gigantism occurs when excess growth hormone acts before epiphyseal growth plates close, allowing excessive linear growth.

**Detailed explanation**

Gigantism occurs when excess growth hormone acts before epiphyseal growth plates close, allowing excessive linear growth. **Why the others are wrong:** (a) Open epiphysis: correct. (b) Open metaphysis: not the relevant growth-plate distinction. (c) Closed metaphysis: irrelevant. (d) Closed epiphysis: associated with acromegaly rather than gigantism.

Curriculum alignment: Endocrine 2025: growth hormone excess, gigantism and acromegaly. **High-yield point / Review Notes:** Excess GH after epiphyseal closure causes acromegalic changes without further linear growth.

High-yield point: Gigantism occurs when excess growth hormone acts before epiphyseal growth plates close, allowing excessive linear growth.

The anterior pituitary secretes ACTH, TSH, FSH, LH, growth hormone and prolactin. ADH is synthesized in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary.

**Detailed explanation**

The anterior pituitary secretes ACTH, TSH, FSH, LH, growth hormone and prolactin. ADH is synthesized in the hypothalamus and released from the posterior pituitary. **Why the others are wrong:** (a) ACTH: anterior pituitary. (b) TSH: anterior pituitary. (c) FSH: anterior pituitary. ## EB-paediatrics-miscellaneous-topics-accepted-Q136 — Q136 — Endocrinology / parathyroid function

**Initial explanation**

In primary hypoparathyroidism, parathyroid hormone secretion is reduced or absent, causing hypocalcaemia and hyperphosphataemia.

**Detailed explanation**

In primary hypoparathyroidism, parathyroid hormone secretion is reduced or absent, causing hypocalcaemia and hyperphosphataemia. **Why the others are wrong:** (a) Decreased: correct. (b) Increased: may occur in pseudohypoparathyroidism or secondary responses, not primary gland failure. (c) No change: incorrect. (d) None: incorrect.

Curriculum alignment: Endocrine 2025: hypoparathyroidism and calcium-phosphate regulation. **High-yield point / Review Notes:** Low PTH causes low calcium and high phosphate; assess magnesium because severe hypomagnesaemia can impair PTH secretion/action.

High-yield point: In primary hypoparathyroidism, parathyroid hormone secretion is reduced or absent, causing hypocalcaemia and hyperphosphataemia.

## EB-paediatrics-miscellaneous-topics-accepted-Q138 — Q138 — Endocrinology / central hypothyroidism

**Source:** Question-bank MD p.164 · printed p.314 · answer-key PDF p.169 · original Q138

**Initial explanation**

In secondary (central) hypothyroidism caused by pituitary failure, TSH may be low or inappropriately normal despite low free T4. This contrasts with primary thyroid failure, where TSH is generally elevated.

**Detailed explanation**

In secondary (central) hypothyroidism caused by pituitary failure, TSH may be low or inappropriately normal despite low free T4. This contrasts with primary thyroid failure, where TSH is generally elevated. **Why the others are wrong:** (a) Secondary to hypopituitarism: correct. (b) Malignancy: not a direct general cause of low TSH in hypothyroidism. (c) Both: incorrect. (d) None: incorrect.

Curriculum alignment: Endocrine 2025: pituitary/hypothalamic causes of hypothyroidism and TSH interpretation. **High-yield point / Review Notes:** Do not use TSH alone to exclude hypothyroidism when pituitary disease is suspected; check free T4.

High-yield point: In secondary (central) hypothyroidism caused by pituitary failure, TSH may be low or inappropriately normal despite low free T4.

## EB-paediatrics-miscellaneous-topics-accepted-Q227 — Q227 — Endocrinology / congenital hypothyroidism

**Source:** Question-bank MD p.168 · printed p.318 · answer-key PDF p.169 · original Q227

**Initial explanation**

In iodine-sufficient settings, thyroid dysgenesis (agenesis, ectopy or hypoplasia) is the most common cause of permanent congenital hypothyroidism. Iodine deficiency remains a major global cause where iodine nutrition is inadequate, but it is not the intended answer in the typical standard paediatric teaching context.

**Detailed explanation**

In iodine-sufficient settings, thyroid dysgenesis (agenesis, ectopy or hypoplasia) is the most common cause of permanent congenital hypothyroidism. Iodine deficiency remains a major global cause where iodine nutrition is inadequate, but it is not the intended answer in the typical standard paediatric teaching context. **Why the others are wrong:** (a) Thyroid dysgenesis: correct in most iodine-sufficient settings. (b) Defective hormone release/synthesis: dyshormonogenesis, a less common cause. (c) Pendred syndrome: a specific inherited cause of thyroid dyshormonogenesis, uncommon. (d) Iodine deficiency: an important global cause but less common in iodine-sufficient populations.

Curriculum alignment: Endocrine 2025: congenital hypothyroidism and thyroid dysgenesis. **High-yield point / Review Notes:** Clarify local iodine status when applying “most common cause” epidemiologically. ## EXTENDED ACCEPTED

High-yield point: In iodine-sufficient settings, thyroid dysgenesis (agenesis, ectopy or hypoplasia) is the most common cause of permanent congenital hypothyroidism.
