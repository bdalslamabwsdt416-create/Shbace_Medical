## EB-paediatrics-miscellaneous-topics-accepted-Q121 — Q121 — Genetics / chromosomal disorders

**Source:** Question-bank MD p.162 · printed p.312 · answer-key PDF p.169 · original Q121

**Initial explanation**

Turner syndrome classically results from complete or partial absence of one X chromosome; the classic karyotype is 45,X (monosomy X). Down syndrome is usually trisomy 21 and Klinefelter syndrome is usually 47,XXY.

**Detailed explanation**

Turner syndrome classically results from complete or partial absence of one X chromosome; the classic karyotype is 45,X (monosomy X). Down syndrome is usually trisomy 21 and Klinefelter syndrome is usually 47,XXY. **Why the others are wrong:** (a) Down syndrome: usually an extra chromosome 21, not whole-chromosome loss. (b) Turner syndrome: correct; monosomy X. (c) Klinefelter syndrome: usually an extra X chromosome in a male. (d) None: incorrect.

Curriculum alignment: Genetics 2024: sex chromosome aneuploidy and Turner syndrome. **High-yield point / Review Notes:** Turner syndrome may be mosaic or structural; not every patient has a uniform 45,X karyotype.

High-yield point: Turner syndrome classically results from complete or partial absence of one X chromosome; the classic karyotype is 45,X (monosomy X).
