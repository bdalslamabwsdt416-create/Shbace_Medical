**Detailed explanation**

In a primary humoral immune response, IgM is the first immunoglobulin class produced. It becomes detectable after a lag of several days; the exact timing depends on antigen, assay and prior exposure. The source key selects 3–4 days, which is an approximate teaching value.

High-yield point: IgM is the first antibody in the primary response; IgG follows after class switching.

## EB-paediatrics-immunology-accepted-Q25 — Q25 — Why maternal BCG-induced cell-mediated immunity is not transferred

**Source:** MCQ د عرفة متقسم · PDF sequence page 39 · printed page 64 · answer-key page 49 · original question 25.

**Initial explanation**

Cell-mediated immunity is mediated by maternal immune cells and T-cell responses; these do not cross the placenta as a transferable immune mechanism. Placental transfer is mainly IgG antibody. Therefore maternal BCG-induced cellular immunity does not provide the infant with equivalent cellular protection.

**Detailed explanation**

Cell-mediated immunity is mediated by maternal immune cells and T-cell responses; these do not cross the placenta as a transferable immune mechanism. Placental transfer is mainly IgG antibody. Therefore maternal BCG-induced cellular immunity does not provide the infant with equivalent cellular protection.

Curriculum alignment: Pediatric vaccination, pp.2–7 (BCG and active immunity); Neonatology 2024–2025, passive maternal immunity; immunology section.

High-yield point: IgG crosses placenta; maternal T lymphocytes are not transferred as functional systemic cellular immunity to the fetus.
