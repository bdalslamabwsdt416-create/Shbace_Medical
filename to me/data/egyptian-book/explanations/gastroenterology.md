**Source:** Question-bank MD page 133 · Printed page 241 · Answer-key PDF page 137 · Original question Q49

**Initial explanation**

Cirrhosis can cause ascites through portal hypertension, renal sodium retention and reduced effective circulating volume. Nephrotic syndrome can cause ascites through severe hypoalbuminemia and altered Starling forces, often with sodium retention. Thus both can cause low-protein, traditionally “transudative” ascites. Modern ascites evaluation favors the serum–ascites albumin gradient (SAAG) and total protein rather than relying only on the transudate/exudate label; nephrotic ascites is usually low-SAAG/low-protein, whereas cirrhotic portal-hypertensive ascites is high-SAAG/low-protein.
