## EB-paediatrics-miscellaneous-topics-accepted-Q65 — Q65 — Rheumatology / inflammatory markers

**Source:** Question-bank MD p.159 · printed p.309 · answer-key PDF p.169 · original Q65

**Initial explanation**

ESR is a nonspecific marker that is often elevated in acute rheumatic fever, connective-tissue diseases, and tuberculosis. Chorea is a neurological manifestation of rheumatic fever; when it occurs as isolated Sydenham chorea it may appear after the acute inflammatory phase, and ESR can be normal. Chorea itself is not a primary inflammatory cause of raised ESR.

**Detailed explanation**

ESR is a nonspecific marker that is often elevated in acute rheumatic fever, connective-tissue diseases, and tuberculosis. Chorea is a neurological manifestation of rheumatic fever; when it occurs as isolated Sydenham chorea it may appear after the acute inflammatory phase, and ESR can be normal. Chorea itself is not a primary inflammatory cause of raised ESR. **Why the others are wrong:** (a) Chorea: intended exception; it is a neurological manifestation and may occur when inflammatory markers have normalized. (b) Acute rheumatic fever: commonly associated with elevated ESR in active disease. (c) Collagen vascular disease: often elevates ESR. (d) Tuberculosis: chronic infection commonly elevates ESR.

Curriculum alignment: Rheumatology 2024: acute rheumatic fever, connective-tissue disease and inflammatory markers; Infectious 2024: tuberculosis. **High-yield point / Review Notes:** In Jones criteria, ESR/CRP are inflammatory minor criteria; isolated chorea is a special presentation and can occur late.

High-yield point: ESR is a nonspecific marker that is often elevated in acute rheumatic fever, connective-tissue diseases, and tuberculosis.
