Original files are unmodified. The application applies playback filters and
volume changes. Aortic stenosis, mitral regurgitation, aortic regurgitation and mitral
stenosis examples are original signals synthesized by sound.js. They are
not patient recordings or clinically validated acoustic models. Timing,
contour and frequency content are simplified for teaching.

The .wav files are PCM 16-bit conversions of the corresponding .ogg
recordings above and retain the same authorship and licenses. No filtering
or trimming was applied. Normal heart sounds were scaled by 0.96804 to
avoid clipping during conversion; the player normalizes playback levels.

Medical references

Simulations are educational approximations. Auscultation recordings and synthetic examples are identified in the module. Gait and reflex teaching draws on Stanford Medicine 25: gait, tendon reflexes, and the Merck Manual. Model and recording files, with their licence notices, are also available from the Medara website under the same asset paths listed above.

Normal breath sound

normalLung.wav: HLS-CMDS, LS/M_N_RUA.wav (normal lung sound, right upper anterior chest).
Authors: Yasaman Torabi, Shahram Shirani, James P. Reilly (McMaster University).
Recorded from a clinical manikin, not a patient. Source WAV unchanged; renamed for the app.
Dataset: https://zenodo.org/records/15376628
Licence: CC BY 4.0 — https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/

rhonchi.wav: LS/F_R_LUA.wav; amplitude multiplied by 23.075352.
pleuralRub.wav: LS/M_PR_RMA.wav; amplitude multiplied by 34.310995.
Authors: Yasaman Torabi, Shahram Shirani, James P. Reilly, McMaster University.
Source: https://zenodo.org/records/15376628 (v1, 9 May 2025).
Licence: CC BY 4.0 https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
Citation: Torabi, Shirani & Reilly, HLS-CMDS, IEEE Data Descriptions (2025), doi:10.1109/IEEEDATA.2025.3566012.
Changes by Medara: renamed and amplitude-normalised to 0.8 peak (PCM16); no trimming, time stretching or synthesis. Playback filters remain active.
Actual WAV headers: mono, 4000 Hz, 15 seconds (the general README says 22050 Hz).
Archive FC/CC filenames correspond to C/G in the CSV, matched by sex and recording location and the README class definitions.
Each recording is reused at listening points; it does not simulate recorded regional variation.

Additional basic findings

Basic findings added in sounds-4:
tricuspidRegurgitation.wav: E_H_83_T.wav, recorded at the tricuspid area.
Source: Fraiwan, Mohammad; Ibnian, Ali; Haider, Nishi (2025), A Dataset of Heart Sound Regurgitation of Patients with Heart Valve Disorders, Mendeley Data v1.
https://doi.org/10.17632/jfbdvjw6sf.1
Dataset licence: CC BY 4.0 https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
Clinical annotation: participant 83, auscultatory finding TR, echo TR grade 3; coexisting MR grade 2. This is not an isolated lesion.
Changes: excerpt 2–20 seconds (initial handling transient excluded), 5 ms edge fades, amplitude multiplied by 7.941351, PCM16. No timing or pitch changes.

pulmonaryStenosis.wav: supplementary audio 7, Heart energy signature spectrogram for cardiovascular diagnosis.
Authors: Vladimir Kudriavtsev, Vladimir Polyshchuk, Douglas L. Roy (2007), BioMedical Engineering OnLine 6:16.
https://doi.org/10.1186/1475-925X-6-16
Audio source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Heart-energy-signature-spectrogram-for-cardiovascular-diagnosis-1475-925X-6-16-S7.ogg
Licence: CC BY 2.0 https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/2.0/
Changes: Ogg decoded to mono PCM16 WAV, amplitude multiplied by 0.813934, 5 ms edge fades. Full 1.5325-second excerpt loops without changing its speed.

Reduced breath sounds: existing normalLung.wav (HLS-CMDS, CC BY 4.0) with point-dependent playback attenuation; no new patient recording.
The three new findings use simplified educational volume maps. These are not measured site-to-site transmission, isolated murmur stems, or a clinical acoustic model.

The pulmonary stenosis excerpt has additional playback headroom to prevent filter clipping.
